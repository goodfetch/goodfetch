# dom-smoothie is the default main-content extractor, not rs-trafilatura

The default chain is dom-smoothie for main content, htmd for Markdown, html5ever/scraper for selectors and fields, and lol-html for the fast streaming read of links, meta tags and title. rs-trafilatura, the R&D's choice, ships as a selectable alternative behind the same extractor interface. On the independent WebMainBench corpus (545 pages) dom-smoothie beats rs-trafilatura by 0.025 word F1 (CI above zero) and runs 5 to 6 times faster; rs-trafilatura's 0.06 lead on WCXB is not confirmed on an independent corpus. The reversal is consistent with a bias in WCXB, whose answer keys were drafted from rs-trafilatura's own output for 504 of 511 pages before review, but that cause was not tested. Decided in [Adopt collection decisions](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/71), on the lab re-run of [PR 87](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/pull/87).

## Considered Options

- **rs-trafilatura as default**: rejected. One maintainer, no commit since April 2026, three bugs filed in October, and machine-learning layers that Python trafilatura does not have. It ships vendored in a fork Goodfetch can patch, pinned to a reviewed version.
- **lol-html for main content**: rejected. It is a streaming rewriter with no document tree and reached only all-body-text quality on WCXB; it reads fields at 0.27 of scraper's time with 99.6% or more agreement, which is what it is adopted for.
- **mdka or html2md for Markdown**: not adopted yet. mdka leads htmd by 0.045 on WebMainBench, short of a formal verdict. htmd (112 dependent crates) is kept and its two measured defects are fixed upstream: code in `<pre>` without `<code>`, and tables with row headers. If mdka still leads by more than 0.03 after those fixes, the converter switches.
  > Note (agent decision, accepted by the owner on 2026-10-22; [#783](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/783)): until htmd takes them upstream, `goodfetch-extractor` carries both fixes as htmd element handlers of its own, each with a fixture that fails without it, and a third the 30 fixtures need, which every converter but html2md lacked: a heading with a `<br>` stays one heading. Offering the fixes upstream is a pull request to htmd's repository, a public action an agent does not take. The 30 fixtures are the Proving ground's `extraction/7` task, judged by Markdown structure as the lab's oracle judged them, with a heading's level, a code block's language and a link's relative or absolute form not counted, and case and paragraphs counted.

## Consequences

- The 30 hand-written Markdown fixtures become a V1 conformance suite.
- The Markdown converters were compared on rs-trafilatura's content HTML; the adopted dom-smoothie then htmd chain is measured again before the switch rule above is applied.
- No surveyed competitor uses a trafilatura-class extractor: Jina and Apify use Readability, Firecrawl a fixed list of selectors to remove, Crawl4AI its own pruning filters, and Crawlee, Scrapy and Stagehand none in their core.
- Before the extractor, the engine removes the elements matched by the cosmetic rules of the Clean-up rules, in every tier; raw HTML, WARC, Run traces, the lol-html link reading above and the user's own selectors see the page untouched (ADR 0047).
  > Note (agent decision, accepted by the owner on 2026-10-28; [#785](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/785)): how much of the page a call reads. `markdown.content: "full"` is the cleaned, composed document's `<body>` as Markdown, read whether or not the page has main content. `"fit"` is the main content's HTML pruned from its outermost blocks inward before htmd writes it: a block (a paragraph, list, list item, table, quotation, figure, `div`, section or the like; never a heading or code) is left out when more than half its text, white space aside, is in `<a href>` links, when it holds an image and no text, or, for a list, when the page holds another list whose links go to the same paths in the same order, each link's target resolved against the document's base URL before its path, query and fragment are compared (the main-content step writes the article's links absolute, the page's stay as written). `html.raw` is the document as it stands: as served on a document tier, and on the browser tier the DOM's serialization with the `<template shadowrootmode>` of each shadow root taken out, byte for byte otherwise, which is what Playwright's `content()` returns; `html.cleaned` is the same after the cosmetic rules; `html.main` is dom-smoothie's content. `text` is the main content's text, a paragraph per block, one blank line apart: a block is an element the HTML Standard's rendering lays out as a block, a list item, a table part or a list box's row; every other element, phrasing or unknown, runs inside its paragraph, a ruby's `rt` reading kept beside its base and its `rp` fallback, which the rendering hides, left out. `main_content` is answered only beside a format read from the main content (`markdown` of the main content or fit, `html.main`, `text`), so a call that asks for none runs no main-content step (owner, 2026-10-28, O28; story 8). The Proving ground's `extraction/8` task holds two pages with every form written by hand; its oracle judges Markdown by structure, HTML by the text of its blocks and text white space aside.
  > Note (agent decision, accepted by the owner on 2026-10-28; [#786](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/786)): links, images, media and tables as values. `links` lists each `<a href>` and `<area href>`, `images` each `<img src>`, `media` each `<video src>`, `<audio src>` and `<source src>` inside either (with `kind` and the `<source>`'s `type`), in document order, read by a lol-html pass, its shadow roots' templates included: links over the document as it stands, no Clean-up rule applied, and images and media, which are extracted content, over the document after the cosmetic rules (ADR 0047; owner, 2026-10-28, D13), a second pass only when the rules changed it. lol-html passes attribute values and text through as written, so the pass decodes their character references as the HTML standard's tokenizer does, with html5ever's table of names (a property test compares it with html5ever's tree). `href` and `src` are kept as decoded; `url` is that value resolved against the document's base URL by the `url` crate's WHATWG parser, `null` when it does not parse; a link's text has its white space collapsed and its `rel` is split into tokens; `alt` is `null` when absent and `""` when empty. A query is encoded as UTF-8 whatever the document's encoding. `srcset`, `poster` and `<track>` are not read. `tables` reads scraper's html5ever tree of the cleaned, composed document: header rows are a `<thead>`'s, else the leading rows whose cells are all `<th>`; body rows follow, a `<tfoot>`'s last; each cell is one string, its text with white space collapsed, a `<br>` read as a space and a nested table left out (it is listed on its own); `colspan` and `rowspan` are not expanded. `page_formats_schema` derives from the serialize contract, so a nullable field such as `url` stays required in a Dataset's schema. The Proving ground's `extraction/9` task holds three hand-written pages (a `<base href>`, a redirect, tables) whose formats its oracle judges as JSON values.
  > Note (agent decision, pending owner review; [#788](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/788)): the setting `content.extractor` takes `dom_smoothie` (the default) or `trafilatura`, at a Run, a Session's or a page's own `settings` (`run.new_page` and `session.new_page` take `settings`, holding only this key for now) and the configuration layers. rs-trafilatura is the crates.io release `=0.2.2` the lab reviewed, not yet a vendored fork: the fork is made the day a patch is needed, through Cargo's `[patch.crates-io]`, with no code change; its debug notes to stderr are switched off by turning debug assertions off for it in the `dev` profile. It runs with its fallback extraction and its least sizes off, with which it writes a short page's headings, lists and tables; with them on, it answered a short page as one run-together paragraph. Every step is held to one composition rule: a document with no text outside its navigation landmarks has no main content, and a step that finds no text in a document that holds some answers what is left of its body outside those landmarks (pitfall D02): rs-trafilatura finds none on a page holding only a list of links. The connection scope comes with the SDKs' `connect()` settings (#1245).

  > Note (agent decision, accepted by the owner on 2026-10-28; [#787](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/787)): metadata and contacts as values. `metadata` is `{ title, description, canonical, lang, open_graph, twitter, json_ld, microdata, meta }`: the head's tags come from the same lol-html pass as links (the document as it stands): the first `<title>` (white space collapsed), the first `<meta name="description">` (name compared ASCII case-insensitively), the first `<link rel="canonical">` resolved against the base URL (`null` when it does not parse) and the root's `lang`; Open Graph and Twitter tags are lists of `{ property, content }` in document order, so a repeated `og:image` keeps each (a `<meta property>` not named `twitter:`, or a `<meta name>` named `og:`, is Open Graph; one named `twitter:` by either attribute is Twitter); `meta` maps every other `<meta name>`, lower-cased, to its first content. JSON-LD and microdata come from scraper's tree of the cleaned, composed document: each `application/ld+json` block that parses (one that does not is left out), and the top-level microdata items by the HTML Standard's crawl and JSON conversion (`itemref` followed, a looping item written `"ERROR"`, items deeper than 128 written `"ERROR"` too, so a hostile page cannot exhaust the stack, and every value past one call's budget written `"ERROR"` as well: items that reach one container through `itemref` each read it again, as the conversion does, so a page of a few kilobytes holds exponentially many items; the budget is 100,000 steps, each element a crawl visits, each node a text value is read from and each value written, a copy under a second `itemprop` name included, and 4 MiB of text values, and past it an item not reached yet has no properties), each item `{ type[], id|null, properties }`. `contacts` is `{ emails, phones, social }`, read from the same tree, each value once where it first appears, with the text it was read from (its link's text, else the value as written): addresses from `mailto:` links (every address of the list, query dropped) and from text by the HTML Standard's valid e-mail syntax; numbers from `tel:` links (always a number; `e164` only when it carries `+` or the root's `lang` has a two-letter region, and libphonenumber's metadata, the `phonenumber` crate, finds it valid there) and from text, where a span of digits and separators is a number only when it carries `+` or the region exists and the number is valid there, as libphonenumber's own matcher finds no national number without a region (G1); social profiles from links whose host is one of a closed list (X and Twitter, LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, GitHub, with `www.`, `m.` and `mobile.`) and whose first path segment is no sharing or login endpoint, and Mastodon by a `rel="me"` link to a `/@name` path on any other host. The list is `SOCIAL_NETWORKS_VERSION` 1 of `goodfetch-extractor`, whose test pins each version's digest. Text in the head, scripts, styles, templates, `noscript` and `textarea` is not read.
  > Note (agent decision, accepted by the owner on 2026-10-28), 2026-10-28, step 7 review of specs #580 and #582 (finding 580-582-5): ticket #787 reads telephone numbers "from `tel:` links and text", `e164` `null` when neither a country code nor a region gives it, so a number in text is no longer dropped for want of a region. A span of digits and separators that carries `+`, or stands in a document whose root `lang` has a region, is still a number only when libphonenumber finds it valid, and gets its E.164. Without either, no country is guessed: every span the scanner reads (7 to 15 digits, from a `+`, `(` or digit after no letter or digit, with spaces, hyphens, dots and parentheses between) is a number with `e164: null`. The Proving ground's `/structured/no-region` page now expects its national number in text with `e164: null`. Owner, 2026-10-28 (O27): with a region or without, a span written as another value is no number: what libphonenumber's matcher leaves out, a date written with digits alone (a four-digit year, a month and a day, joined by one hyphen, dot or space each, in either order) and a span an `:mm` follows (a time stamp's date and hour); a range of two years from 1800 to 2199 joined by a hyphen, the first before the second; a word of digits, one dot and digits (decimals, such as coordinates); and an ISBN or an EAN whose check digit holds, written as one: 13 digits beginning with `978` or `979`, 13 digits written together (`00` first, a number dialled from abroad, aside), or an ISBN-10 in hyphenated groups whose check digit is a group of its own. A telephone number is rarely written as a product code, so a number whose digits happen to check stays one.
