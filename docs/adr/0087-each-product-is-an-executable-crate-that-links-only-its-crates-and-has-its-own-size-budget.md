# Each Product is an executable crate that links only its crates, and has its own Size budget

ADR 0084 decided that Goodfetch ships two Products, Collie and Foxhound, whose executable contains Collie's code, from one repository. This decides how they are built. Each Product is one executable crate of the one Cargo workspace, linking only the crates its capabilities need and built by its own `cargo build -p`. Every crate only Foxhound links lives under `crates/foxhound/`, and two cargo-deny checks prove that nothing else but the `xtask` build tool depends on it. Foxhound adds to the engine through values that each executable crate composes, and to a crawl handler's page through a navigation gate and a lease check it sets at hand-over; a request for what Collie lacks fails with `not_in_product`, naming Foxhound. Each Product has its own Size budget. The research behind it ([multi-product builds](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/blob/research/multi-product-builds/rnd/research/2026-10-14-multi-product-builds.md), rechecked 2026-10-15 where cited) found:
- **In the closest precedents, a larger product that links a smaller one is built from separate packages, not from Cargo features.** `codex-cli` depends on the `codex-app-server` crate, which has its own `[[bin]]`; `uv` contains the build backend that ships alone as `uv_build`; `deno` and `denort` share `deno_lib`. Each smaller product has its own build invocation, since Cargo merges features across the packages of one invocation ([cargo#4463](https://github.com/rust-lang/cargo/issues/4463); the fix is still unstable, [cargo#14774](https://github.com/rust-lang/cargo/issues/14774)).
- **The other mechanisms drew the failures ADRs 0038 and 0057 predicted.** spider-rs's 129 Cargo features shipped broken combinations ([#349](https://github.com/spider-rs/spider/issues/349)), and Homebrew dropped build options that "could not be tested on our CI system" ([Homebrew 2.0.0](https://brew.sh/2019/02/02/homebrew-2.0.0/)). `deno compile`'s download of `denort` fails in CI ([deno#27988](https://github.com/denoland/deno/issues/27988)). Codex's V8 host, a companion executable, was left out of a package ([#38975](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/38975)), shipped unnotarized ([#47521](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/47521)) and skewed from its client ([#42111](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/42111)) within three months.
- **A per-product dependency check is cheap and in production.** uv runs `cargo deny --manifest-path crates/uv-build/Cargo.toml --all-features check bans` against a `deny.toml` that bans `rustls`, `openssl`, `reqwest` and `schemars` ([check-lint.yml](https://github.com/astral-sh/uv/blob/main/.github/workflows/check-lint.yml), [deny.toml](https://github.com/astral-sh/uv/blob/main/crates/uv-build/deny.toml)). Codex bans `reqwest` except under listed direct parents, first-party crates among them, with cargo-deny's `wrappers` ([deny.toml](https://github.com/openai/codex/blob/main/codex-rs/deny.toml)).
- **A probe for this decision** (cargo-deny 0.20.2, Rust 1.93.0, a workspace of path crates, 2026-10-15): the check rooted at Collie's executable alone missed a shared crate that Collie does not link depending on a Foxhound crate, and, once it leaves out dev-dependencies as its bans of test-only crates require, any dev-dependency on one; a workspace-wide `wrappers` rule caught both and passed the clean graph.

Decided in [How the products are built, and each product's Size budget](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/454), on the map [Goodfetch products: Collie and Foxhound as separately installable products](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/447), by the agent on the owner's delegation of 2026-10-14 (rounds 1 to 3, an addendum, a correction, and round 4 with its refinements on the ticket). What Collie carries is [Collie's exact capability set](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/455)'s (ADR 0088); this ADR places its answers in the crate graph and sizes them.

## Decision

### One executable crate per Product

- **Each Product is one executable crate** in the one Cargo workspace, linking only the crates its capabilities need, so what a Product contains is its dependency graph.
- **Each is built by its own `cargo build -p` per target** (ADR 0084), with ADR 0057's one release profile.
- **No Cargo feature selects a Product,** and none switches a capability within one (ADR 0038, amended below).
- **This adds no companion executable and no download on first use.** The existing ones are unchanged: the sink processes (ADR 0026), the Linux sandbox helper and the Windows filter broker (ADR 0033), browsers, Plugins and the Clean-up rules' daily channel (ADRs 0024, 0033, 0047).
- **Each executable embeds only the data its own capabilities use:** its own Console build, its own offline docs and `init` starters, and the Clean-up rules, which both Products render pages with.

### Two sides of the crate graph

- **Every crate only Foxhound links lives under `crates/foxhound/`:**
  - Foxhound's executable crate;
  - the crates of what ADR 0084 and Collie's exact capability set make Foxhound's: the Frontier with its work-item leases, crawl scope, the Session pool, Politeness's per-domain admission and robots.txt gate, the HTTP and discovered-API fetch tiers with impersonated HTTP and the choice of tier per route, Paid fetches, Datasets and Records with their change tracking, Exports, Sinks, DuckDB with search across runs and the SQL tab, and Partitioned crawls;
  - the sink processes, moved from `crates/sinks/` to `crates/foxhound/sinks/`.
- **Everything else stays under `crates/` and may be linked by Collie:** Collie's crates, the pieces both Products carry, `goodfetch-protocol` and the test-only crates. There is no third group, because the pieces both Products carry may depend on Collie's crates; the area 3 spec's engine already drives browsers through Collie's CDP driver.
- **Foxhound carries everything Collie carries by construction.** Collie's executable crate exposes its composition (below) as a library beside its `main`, and Foxhound's executable crate starts from it, as `codex-cli` links `codex-app-server`.
- **The published Rust crates through which each Product's SDK embeds its engine** (ADR 0024) sit on their Product's side. How many there are is [Protocol, Operation catalog and SDKs across two products](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/458)'s; their names, and every crate's and executable's, are [Product names, packages, executables and version line](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/457)'s.
  > Amended by [ADR 0090](0090-products-are-named-goodfetch-collie-and-goodfetch-foxhound-and-share-one-product-version.md): the crate a Product publishes as its Rust SDK is `goodfetch-collie` or `goodfetch-foxhound`; its executable crate, when that is not the published crate, and any further crate it publishes for its users is `goodfetch-<product>-<part>`; the executables are `goodfetch-collie` and `goodfetch-foxhound`. Every other crate, those under `crates/foxhound/` included, keeps `goodfetch-<glossary term>`.

  > Amended by [ADR 0095](0095-both-products-speak-one-protocol-from-one-catalog-marked-by-product-and-their-sdks-share-collies-client-packages.md): Collie publishes two crates, `goodfetch-collie-client`, the client with Collie's values and no engine, and `goodfetch-collie`, its Rust SDK with the linked engine; Foxhound publishes `goodfetch-foxhound`. Both SDK crates depend on `goodfetch-collie-client` at exactly their own version.

### What CI checks

Every pull request runs the first three, each in seconds; the fourth runs where ADR 0057 puts it.

1. **The direction, workspace-wide.** No crate outside `crates/foxhound/` depends on a crate inside it, as a normal, build or dev dependency, except `xtask`: the build tool, never shipped, may read every Product's types to generate code (ADR 0038). cargo-deny bans each Foxhound crate with `wrappers` listing Foxhound's own crates and `xtask` as its only allowed direct parents; `xtask codegen` writes the list from the folder, and CI fails when the committed list differs.
2. **What each Product ships, rooted at its executable crate with `--all-features` and `--exclude-dev`** (uv's pattern, ADR 0084). Dev-dependencies are left out because tests take the test-only crates; the workspace-wide check covers them.
   - Collie's bans Foxhound's crates and the third-party crates only Foxhound's capabilities use, starting with DuckDB's crates, `wreq` and its BoringSSL, `x402-rs` and `alloy`, and the in-binary Sinks' database and object-store drivers. A third-party crate that only Foxhound's crates use joins the list in the pull request that adds it, and the fresh review checks it.
     > Amended by [ADR 0124](0124-collies-tls-relay-links-wreqs-boringssl-but-not-wreq-and-forwards-the-browsers-own-http2.md): Collie's TLS relay links BoringSSL through `btls`, `btls-sys` and `tokio-btls`, the fork `wreq` uses, at `wreq`'s version. Collie's list therefore bans `wreq` and the crates that exist for it (`wreq-proto`, `wreq-rt`, `http2`, `wreq-util`), not its BoringSSL. `btls`, `btls-sys` and `tokio-btls` are each a deny entry whose `wrappers` list its allowed direct parents: `tokio-btls` the relay's crate; `btls` the relay's crate and `tokio-btls`; `btls-sys` `btls`, and the relay's crate only if it calls the FFI itself.
     > Amended by [ADR 0133](0133-agent-work-follows-the-matt-pocock-skills-as-written-and-the-fresh-review-is-retired.md): no fresh review exists; this check is a review standard the `code-review` skill applies, and the owner sees its result before merging.
   - Each Product's bans the test-only crates: `goodfetch-test-support`, which builds the Scripted engine, and the crate of turmoil's network adapter (below).
     > Amended by [ADR 0125](0125-a-test-adapter-of-a-goodfetch-interface-is-its-own-test-only-crate-which-no-unit-test-of-a-crate-it-depends-on-takes.md): the bans cover every test adapter crate on ADR 0125's list, `goodfetch-simulation` and `goodfetch-scripted-model` to start with, beside `goodfetch-test-support`.
3. **The Console's packages** (below): Collie's Console build fails to resolve a package import of Foxhound's screens, and the Console's lint configuration refuses a relative import that leaves its package.
4. **Each Product's Size budget** (below), the backstop on the built files.

### How Foxhound adds to the engine

- **Each domain crate exports one value listing what it adds to the engine:** operation handlers, settings keys, run-state tables, Plugin kinds and Hook stages, Usage kinds and Limit scopes, the Notifications it can send, and its gates in admission.
- **Each Product's executable crate passes the values it links to the engine at start**, so the composition is a list in `main` and nothing registers itself by being linked. Deno builds its runtime the same way, from the extensions passed in `RuntimeOptions.extensions` ([jsruntime.rs](https://github.com/denoland/deno_core/blob/main/core/runtime/jsruntime.rs)), and `deno` and `denort` pass different lists. Foxhound's composition is Collie's plus Foxhound's values.
- **Code outside `crates/foxhound/` never names a Foxhound type** (`xtask` excepted). Where Foxhound adds behaviour to the engine, the seam is an interface of the engine, such as the gate in admission that Foxhound's per-domain queues and per-domain Politeness fill, or the page's value below. It is a real seam under `STYLE.md`'s two-adapter rule, because the two Products compose different sets.
  > Amended by [ADR 0099](0099-every-surface-spells-each-catalog-name-by-its-own-rule-shapes-come-from-declared-kinds-and-call-is-the-way-down.md): Foxhound's contract types (its operations' parameters and results, events, Error codes and their details, and the values it adds to shared types) are data, defined in `goodfetch-protocol`'s `foxhound` module beside every other contract, so one crate still emits the one bundle and catalog file (ADR 0038). Outside `crates/foxhound/`, only `goodfetch-protocol` and `xtask` name that module, and a check beside the boundary checks above fails when another crate does.
- **Each executable crate tests that its composition has exactly one handler for each operation of its Product's catalog** (the catalogs are #458's), and Foxhound's that its composition contains all of Collie's. Tests and the Simulation (ADR 0060) compose the engine from the values they need.
- **A crawl handler's page carries what Foxhound's handler context sets when it hands the page over.** Collie's page has an optional value for it, an interface of Collie's crates with four parts, and a page a program opens leaves it empty.
  > Note (accepted by the owner on 2026-10-28, the deep review's O4): the value has four parts, each with its own consumer: the navigation gate and the lease check below, the Page check the crawl request's Label declares, which judges the page in place of its Session's (ADR 0093's #774 note), and the main-content step the Label's `content.extractor` chooses, which `page.formats` reads with (ADR 0007's extractor note). The two later parts were added beside the first two rather than behind one of them, since that indirection would change no behaviour.
  - **The navigation gate** (ADR 0086). The engine consults it where it already intercepts every request of the page (ADRs 0014, 0017, 0046): for each main-frame navigation, whatever starts it (a `goto`, a click, the page's own JavaScript, an AI verb), and each fetch made through the page's `request`. The permit is held until the response arrives, for a browser-tier page until its load event. Foxhound backs it with the per-domain limits and delay, the backoff and robots.txt.
    > Amended by [ADR 0118](0118-every-fetch-tier-follows-redirects-as-the-browser-does-fails-a-cut-body-and-resends-only-what-a-site-never-processed.md): a permit covers one hop. Each redirect or refresh hop takes its own permit, and the 3xx or refresh stub that ends a hop releases its permit, so only the last hop's permit is held until the response arrives, or the load event on a browser-tier page. The load of an admitted crawl request takes no permit for its first hop, which admission gave, and one for each hop after it, so the "no second permit" below covers only that first hop.
    > Amended by [ADR 0121](0121-service-workers-run-under-the-engines-interception-the-back-forward-cache-is-off-locators-reach-closed-shadow-roots-and-a-read-never-scrolls-a-list.md): the gate counts what reaches the site. On Chromium, a service worker's request that carries a page's navigation to the network takes that navigation's permit, though the engine sees it on the service worker's target; a navigation a service worker answers from its own cache sends nothing to the site and takes none. The back/forward cache is off, so `goBack` and `goForward` always reach the gate as navigations.
  - **The lease check** (ADR 0003). The engine asks it before each outgoing browser or network command of the page, so a handler past its work item's lease sends nothing more, and the raw CDP relay binds a handler's attach to it and ends the attach with `attach_ended` when the lease ends. Foxhound backs it with the work item's lease.
    > Note (agent decision, pending owner review), 2026-10-23, [#720](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/720): the value is `goodfetch_page::page_value::PageValue`. Its gate is a `NavigationGate` whose response-time method is `judge_document(response) -> Result<(), Refusal>`, given the document's status, headers and body before the page renders it (spec #577's **(shape)**). Its lease check is `goodfetch_engine::lease::LeaseCheck`, which gains `permit_wait(url)`: the page holds its answer through each wait for a permit, and that is where Foxhound renews the lease and names the wait in the Run's live status. Foxhound sets both, and registers its response observer, through one hook, `HandlerPages`, which `goodfetch_page::contribution_with` takes and asks as each page of a Session opens; `goodfetch_page::contribution` passes the empty one. A command the lease check withholds raises `attempt_ended`. A refused navigation is failed as `net::ERR_ABORTED`, so the page keeps its document. Each hop of a `page.request` takes its own permit, released when its response arrives.
    > Note (agent decision, pending owner review), 2026-10-24, [#760](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/760): until the browser tier hands a handler its own page (#767), Foxhound sets the value through the fetch tiers' `gate` module: while an attempt's handler runs, it hands its Run's pages over, and a page that opens in the Run's Sessions meanwhile takes the value of the attempt handed over last (with `crawl.handler.concurrency` above 1, the latest attempt's lease). A request to the attempt's own domain key goes under the Permit its admission holds, waiting `politeness.domain.delay` and the Crawl-delay since the domain's last start; any other key takes a Permit beside the Run's requests, as a hedge's second copy does. robots.txt is read through the copy the user's runs share, and a page's wait renews the lease and shows the request `waiting_for_permit` in the Run's live status.
    > Note (agent decision, pending owner review), 2026-10-25, [#892](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/892): the gate refuses a host whose Crawl-delay is above `site_preferences.max_crawl_delay` with `crawl_delay`, after a Disallow, and reads no robots.txt while `site_preferences.respect_robots_txt` is off. A request on the attempt's own domain key, or after a robots.txt it fetched, takes its start in the Politeness file at once (the Frontier's `start_after`) rather than reading the last start and recording its own after the wait, so concurrent handlers never go together; a request on another key keeps its Permit's spacing. A page opened by a program's `run.new_page` still takes the attempt handed over last, so the eight-handler conformance case adds each seed once the handler before it opened its page; #767's handed page ends that stand-in.
    > Note (agent decision, pending owner review), 2026-10-25, [#761](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/761): a handler's page moves to `Closing` (spec #594's `LeaseEnded`, P31) when its lease check first withholds a command of one of its calls, the call raising the lease check's error, Foxhound's `attempt_ended` (the code #720 chose, which area 6 owns beside area 3's `lease_lost`); the engine then closes its target whatever the lease, and its later calls raise `closed`. The end is taken when the check refuses rather than at the lease's instant, so the call that finds the lease ended says why, and a page that sends nothing more after its lease ends stays open until its attempt or its Run closes it. A page a program opens has no lease check and is never ended by one.
  - It is set after the crawl request that loaded the page was admitted, so that load takes no second permit. Every page opened from that page, or in its Session by the handler, while the handler runs (a popup, a new tab) carries the same value; how such pages are opened is [Collie's page API](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/472)'s and [Crawl definition and handler context](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/477)'s.
  - Collie's executable therefore contains the value's interface, none of the per-domain Politeness and no work-item lease. ADR 0053's per-proxy limits and their sharing, which Collie's exact capability set gives Collie, hold a program's navigation through a proxy on Collie's side, outside this seam, as the run epoch fences a Collie run.
  - How a refused navigation surfaces in the handler, and whether the wait for a permit counts against `timeout.navigation` and the handler's timeout, are Crawl definition and handler context's. What the gate does on a page lent to a raw CDP client, which can switch off the engine's interception (ADR 0014), is [Third-party automation on Foxhound pages](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/456)'s.
    > Amended by [ADR 0089](0089-a-page-is-lent-through-an-endpoint-that-shows-only-its-session-and-collie-joins-other-crawlers-by-borrowing.md): on a page lent to a raw CDP client, a Lent page, the gate keeps gating the lent page and the pages opened from it, and the pages the library creates through the endpoint carry it too; where interception no longer sees the library's requests (ADR 0047's case), they go ungated and the Run trace says so. A library's own HTTP client, such as Playwright's `request`, sends from the program's process and never reaches the gate.
    > Amended by [ADR 0093](0093-a-crawl-declares-its-labels-and-one-handler-each-and-a-handler-reads-collies-page-on-every-fetch-tier.md): a navigation the gate refuses raises `site_preferences_refused` with its reason from the call or the waiter that asked for it; one no call waits on is aborted, the page keeps its document, and it is a `requestfailed` event recorded in the Run trace. A permit's wait counts against neither `timeout.navigation` nor `timeout.handler`. On the document tiers, a handler's `goto`, `reload` and `session().newPage()` are served over the same tier through the gate; a popup escalates to the browser.
  - Crawlee checks robots.txt where a request enters its queue (`enqueueLinks`) or leaves it (its context pipeline), and on its main branch paces domains in a `ThrottlingRequestManager` wrapped around the queue ([basic-crawler.ts](https://github.com/apify/crawlee/blob/master/packages/basic-crawler/src/internals/basic-crawler.ts)); its handlers get a plain Playwright `Page` ([browser-crawler.ts](https://github.com/apify/crawlee/blob/master/packages/browser-crawler/src/internals/browser-crawler.ts)), so a handler's `page.goto` passes neither. ADR 0086 rejected that gap.
- **Simulation scenarios live beside the behaviour they test.** Those that need a Foxhound crate (Partition handoff, the crawl's work-item leases, Sink delivery) are integration tests of the Foxhound crate that holds the behaviour, in its `tests/it/simulation/`, compiled under `cfg(tokio_unstable)` in the same job as `goodfetch-engine`'s. turmoil's network adapter moves out of `goodfetch-engine`'s `tests/it` into a test-only crate of its own outside `crates/foxhound/`, which both sides take as a dev-dependency. It depends on `goodfetch-engine`, whose network interface it implements, so only integration tests take it, where the engine is linked once: a unit test of `goodfetch-engine` would see two copies of the engine's types, and one rule for every crate is simpler (ADR 0062). The "Test infrastructure" spec names it.
  > Amended by [ADR 0125](0125-a-test-adapter-of-a-goodfetch-interface-is-its-own-test-only-crate-which-no-unit-test-of-a-crate-it-depends-on-takes.md): the rule for turmoil's adapter is now ADR 0125's, for every test adapter of a Goodfetch interface: no unit test of a crate the adapter depends on takes it. turmoil's adapter is still taken only by integration tests, because Simulation scenarios are integration tests.
- **The Console is one codebase with one build per Product.**
  - Foxhound's screens (Datasets with the SQL tab, Sinks, crawl progress, search across runs) are a workspace package of their own under `console/`, which Collie's Console build does not depend on.
  - pnpm's default layout links "only the direct dependencies" of a package ([pnpm](https://pnpm.io/motivation)), so Collie's build cannot import them as a package, as Cargo refuses an undeclared crate; a lint rule closes the relative path.
  - Each Product's executable embeds its own Console build. Playwright builds its trace viewer and UI mode the same way, as entries of one package ([vite.config.ts](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/blob/main/packages/trace-viewer/vite.config.ts)).

### A request for what the running Product lacks

Foxhound contains Collie, so only a Collie engine meets one: a Foxhound SDK or a raw Client on a Collie Daemon, a Recipe written for Foxhound, a `crawl.*` setting, `crawl` typed on Collie's CLI.
> Amended by [ADR 0096](0096-the-browser-host-runs-from-either-products-executable-and-goodfetch-cloud-runs-each-run-on-the-product-it-needs.md): Foxhound's engine also raises `not_in_product` for an operation, Plugin kind or Hook stage that only Foxhound carries, called or enabled on the run of a Recipe whose manifest names Collie, whether the Recipe's code or a Client does it (a setting key there keeps `setting_not_applicable`, as on any Collie Run); for such a Recipe on either engine, its details name the Recipe and its manifest's Product, and its `fix` says to name Foxhound in the manifest, and only on a person's own Collie engine also to run it with Foxhound. Behind Goodfetch Cloud's gateway, any other call on a run of Collie's engine names the run's Product, and its `fix` says that only a run started for Foxhound carries the call.

- **The Error code is `not_in_product`,** an `UnavailableError` (fix the setup, ADR 0079), not retryable. Its details name the running Product with its version and the Product that carries the request. Its `fix` names Foxhound and the install command for the channel the running Product came from, with #457's package names.
  - Example message: "`crawl.start` is part of Foxhound, and this engine is Collie 1.4.0. Fix: run it with Foxhound, which contains everything Collie does (`<install command>`), or connect to a Foxhound engine."
  - ripgrep ("PCRE2 is not available in this build of ripgrep"), Cargo ("no such command", then "help: find a package to install") and Playwright (the exact install command) name the fix the same way.

  > Note (agent decision, accepted by the owner on 2026-10-28), 2026-10-26, [#1199](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/1199): the executable learns its channel from the deployment variable `GOODFETCH_CHANNEL` (`npm`, `pypi`, `crates-io`, `homebrew`, `oci` or `installer`), which the channel's launcher, wrapper or image sets; else from a release build's stamp of the same name, which the release pipeline sets at build time (the installer archives); else it is a build from source, crates.io. Another value stops the executable with `setting_invalid`. Collie's executable refuses a command line it cannot read that names an operation only Foxhound carries (`crawl start`, `run add-seed`), a command of the table's `commands` section, or a namespace only those use (`crawl`) with `not_in_product`, exit `10`, before any engine starts; anything else it cannot read stays a usage error, exit `2`. The details keep area 3's shape (`product`, `product_version`, `needs`, `requested`, `fix`), not spec #608's `{ engineProduct, engineVersion, neededProduct, recipe }`, so every `not_in_product` has one shape; the owner accepted this shape on 2026-10-28 (deep review's O18). Whether a Recipe's run's refusal names its Recipe in these details, as ADR 0096's amendment above says, and under which key, is decided before [#1691](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/1691) lands.

  > Amended by [ADR 0095](0095-both-products-speak-one-protocol-from-one-catalog-marked-by-product-and-their-sdks-share-collies-client-packages.md): a Daemon group's peer handshake also refuses a Daemon of the other Product with `not_in_product`, so a Foxhound Daemon meets one when it joins a Collie group. There the details name both Products, and the `fix` says to start that Daemon with the group's Product.
- **An explicit request is refused:** an operation; a CLI command, with the exit code of ADR 0069's table; a value in `settings`, a flag, a form or a Recipe; a Recipe step; enabling a Plugin kind or Hook stage the Product lacks.
- **A shared file is read without it.** User and project configuration and Operator policy may hold the other Product's keys, because one project or one Goodfetch home can serve both Products. A Product applies its own keys, and `config explain` lists the others as Foxhound's, so none is hidden. A key neither Product knows stays `setting_unknown` (ADR 0083).
- **How an engine knows.** `xtask codegen` writes, into a crate both Products link, the names of every operation, setting key, CLI command, Plugin kind and Hook stage that only Foxhound carries: names only, whatever shape #458 gives the catalogs. The settings schema (ADR 0028) records, for each key, the Products that honour it. On a Collie engine this check comes before ADR 0083's scope check, so `crawl.*` there answers `not_in_product`, not `setting_not_applicable`.
  > Amended by [ADR 0095](0095-both-products-speak-one-protocol-from-one-catalog-marked-by-product-and-their-sdks-share-collies-client-packages.md): the names come from the mark each entry of `goodfetch-protocol` carries, the smallest Product that carries it, and also cover every value Foxhound adds to a type both Products share (a Usage kind, a Limit scope, a Notification event), which a Collie engine refuses and lists as Foxhound's as it does these keys.
  > Amended by [ADR 0099](0099-every-surface-spells-each-catalog-name-by-its-own-rule-shapes-come-from-declared-kinds-and-call-is-the-way-down.md): `goodfetch-protocol`, which both Products link, also holds Foxhound's contract types in its `foxhound` module (see above), so it carries more than Foxhound's names; Collie's crates never name that module.

### Each Product's Size budget

| What is measured | Foxhound ceiling | Collie ceiling | Increase threshold per pull request |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compressed release file per platform: the npm platform package, the wheel and the GitHub Release archive | 60 MB | 50 MB | +1 MB |
| Raw executable per platform | 160 MB | 120 MB | — |
| Each published crate's tarball (`.crate`) | 8 MiB | 8 MiB | — |
| Each OCI image tag (ADR 0049) | none | none | +3 MB compressed |

- **Foxhound keeps ADR 0057's numbers.** ADR 0084 moved nothing out of what was the one binary.
- **Collie's ceilings come from its components,** measured on macOS arm64 with thin LTO, stripped, before the engine's own code (research §7, ADR 0057):
  - the CDP client, SQLite, HTTP with rustls, the HTTP stack that serves the Console, and the extractor, measured as one binary: 16.0 MB raw, 6.8 MB compressed;
  - the filter list engine of the Clean-up rules: +2.6 MB raw, +1.0 MB compressed;
  - the embedded Clean-up rules and Console: +4.1 MB, already compressed;
  - V8 for the Script sandbox, which Collie's exact capability set puts in Collie: +42.8 MB prebuilt, up to about +60 MB built from source with its sandbox (Codex's V8 host is 65.4–74.0 MB), about +15 MB compressed prebuilt and about +21 MB from source (the prebuilt ratio, scaled);
  - so 65–83 MB raw and 27–33 MB compressed, or 73–93 MB and 30–37 MB with static musl's 12% more.
- **Neither Product counts the browser.** The Goodfetch Chromium stays outside every executable (ADR 0084), and its archive keeps the Chromium build gate's own 5 MB threshold (ADR 0074).
- **The headroom absorbs the engine's own code,** which no probe counted: 13–20 MB compressed and 27–47 MB raw over Collie's projection, as ADR 0057's 15–25 MB did for the full binary. The ceilings stop drift; a misplaced Foxhound dependency, such as DuckDB (+33.0 MB raw), is stopped earlier by Collie's ban list.
  > Amended by [ADR 0124](0124-collies-tls-relay-links-wreqs-boringssl-but-not-wreq-and-forwards-the-browsers-own-http2.md): Collie's parts also include BoringSSL for its TLS relay, measured at +1.43 MB raw and +0.70 MB compressed. That leaves about 12–19 MB compressed and 26–46 MB raw of headroom, with musl, and no ceiling moves.
- **One increase threshold, +1 MB,** is about 3% of Collie's compressed file and 2–3% of Foxhound's, so a change to a crate both Products link trips both checks or neither.
- **ADR 0057's rules hold per Product:**
  - every target is measured, and the largest must fit;
  - the increase check compares each Product's build with `main`'s build of the same Product and profile;
  - the ceilings are checked on the release-profile files before any publish;
  - a pull request that trips a check passes with a "Size increase" section the fresh review accepts on its exact head;
    > Amended by [ADR 0133](0133-agent-work-follows-the-matt-pocock-skills-as-written-and-the-fresh-review-is-retired.md): no fresh review exists; the owner accepts the section when merging.
  - a ceiling moves only by changing the ADR that sets it, with the owner's approval: ADR 0057 for Foxhound's, this ADR for Collie's.
- **ADR 0071's Console ceiling** (1 MB brotli) and increase check apply to each Product's Console build.
- Competitors at Collie's job: agent-browser is one 15.8–18.3 MB Rust binary, Playwright installs 18.3 MB, Stagehand v4's npm install is 33.0 MB, and Playwright's Python wheels are 33–46 MiB ([browser-automation products](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/blob/research/browser-automation-products/rnd/research/2026-10-14-browser-automation-products.md)). The browser every Collie user downloads is 124–198 MB compressed (ADR 0057).

## Considered Options

- **Products as Cargo features of one crate** (`--features collie|foxhound`): rejected. Code would carry `cfg` branches for each Product, a combination matrix would need testing, and Cargo's feature unification would put Foxhound's dependencies into a Collie built in the same invocation. spider-rs and Homebrew show the failure, and ADR 0038 already rejected capabilities as features.
- **`cfg(feature = "foxhound")` inside the crates both Products link:** rejected for the same reasons, at a smaller scale.
- **Collie as the base, with Foxhound's parts downloaded on first use:** rejected. It brings ADR 0057's costs (a wait, offline failures, a signed and pinned artifact per part, proxies and mirrors) and Deno's download failures, and Foxhound would not work offline out of the box.
- **Foxhound's parts as companion executables beside Collie's:** rejected. Codex's V8 host shows the costs, and ADR 0084 already refused a process boundary between Collie and Foxhound for fencing and the Effect journal. **As process Plugins** (ADR 0033): rejected for the same boundary.
- **Three groups of crates** (shared, Collie's, Foxhound's): rejected. It adds a direction ADR 0084 does not ask for, keeping shared crates off Collie's, which the engine's use of Collie's CDP driver would break.
- **A crate-name prefix or a manifest metadata mark as the boundary:** rejected. Names are #457's and a rule on names breaks when they change; a metadata mark is invisible in the tree.
- **ADR 0084's per-Product check alone:** rejected. The probe shows it misses a shared crate that Collie does not link, and dev-dependencies once it leaves them out, as it must.
- **An allowlist of every crate Collie may use,** as rustc's `tidy` keeps 220: rejected. Every new shared dependency would need a list edit, where the bans already cover what matters.
- **A symbol check on each release binary,** as uv's trampolines run `cargo bloat`: rejected. It needs an unstripped release build per Product and matches mangled names, while the crate graph is exact for Rust code.
- **Self-registration at link time** (`inventory`, `linkme`): rejected. A Product's content would be whatever the linker kept, through a global registry, against ADR 0059's values.
- **Foxhound wrapping the page it hands a handler,** taking permits and checking the lease in its methods: rejected. A click that navigates, the page's own JavaScript or an AI verb would bypass it, and the handler's page would no longer be Collie's object.
- **Per-domain Politeness in Collie, used only by crawls:** rejected. ADR 0084 places it in Foxhound, and Collie's executable would carry code only Foxhound uses.
- **A gate on every request of the Session:** rejected. It would count subresources, which ADR 0086 does not count.
- **One Console build in both executables, hiding Foxhound's screens on Collie:** rejected. Collie would ship Foxhound's code in its Console, and the boundary would hold for Rust only.
- **What a Collie engine says without this decision** (JSON-RPC's "method not found", `setting_unknown`, the CLI's usage error): rejected. It tells the user their program is wrong when the fix is the other Product.
- **Ignoring the other Product's settings, or warning and continuing:** rejected by ADR 0083 (G3) for explicit values. **Refusing configuration files that hold them:** rejected; installing Foxhound beside Collie would break Collie on a file both read.
- **Collie built with `opt-level = "z"`,** as uv builds `uv_build`: rejected for performance. ADR 0057 measured `"s"` and `"z"` 18% and 90% slower on its query, LLRT measured 7% for `"s"`, and Collie's hot paths (CDP messages, the guard, extraction) are what ADR 0071 counts. It would save a quarter of Collie's compressed file, a few MB beside the browser.
- **One set of ceilings for both Products:** rejected. Collie's would never bind.
- **Collie's ceilings set only after its first measured build:** rejected. Collie would have no hard stop until then, the gap ADR 0057 closed with numbers up front.

## Consequences

- **Amends ADR 0038.** Within a Product a capability is a setting, never a Cargo feature, and the Products differ only by the crates their executables link. Each Product's executable builds one fixed feature set in its own `cargo build -p`; features remain only on the published crates through which each Product's SDK embeds its engine, for heavy optional dependencies and test utilities, additive, with `cargo-hack` over each crate's combinations. Notes sit under its capabilities item and its rejected option "Capabilities as Cargo features".
- **Amends ADR 0057.** Each capability is in the executable of every Product that carries it, and the Size budget, its tiers and its rules apply per Product, with Collie's ceilings set here. Notes sit under its "What stays in the binary" item and under its table.
- **Amends ADR 0032.** The tree gains `crates/foxhound/`, with the sink processes in `crates/foxhound/sinks/`, and the pull-request tier runs the boundary checks. Notes sit under the tree and under the pull-request tier's dependency checks.
- **Amends ADR 0060.** Simulation scenarios that need a Foxhound crate live in that crate's `tests/it/simulation/`, and turmoil's network adapter in a test-only crate outside `crates/foxhound/`. Notes sit under its "Simulation scenarios" item and its `--cfg tokio_unstable` item.
- **Amends ADR 0062.** A second test-only crate, holding turmoil's network adapter, depends on `goodfetch-engine` and is taken only by integration tests. A note sits under its rule that one crate holds every shared helper.
- **Amends ADR 0086.** The seam it left to this ticket is the navigation gate above, which every page opened from a handler's page, or in its Session by the handler, carries too; on a Collie engine, a key of its table that only Foxhound honours fails with `not_in_product`, its `setting_not_applicable` staying for a Collie Run on a Foxhound engine. Notes sit under its crawl requests, its Collie Runs and its list of what it left to other tickets.
- **Amends ADR 0071.** Its Console ceiling and increase check apply to each Product's Console build. A note sits under the Console ceiling.
- **Amends ADR 0083.** The product check comes before the scope check, so a `crawl.*` setting on a Collie engine is `not_in_product`; a configuration file may hold the other Product's keys, which `config explain` lists; and every build of a Product has the same types. Notes sit under its refusal table and the paragraph after it.
- **Error codes.** `not_in_product` joins `goodfetch-protocol` in the `unavailable` group, not retryable, with an exit code in ADR 0069's table. The settings schema gains, per key, the Products that honour it.
- **`STYLE.md`.** Members live under `crates/`, `crates/foxhound/` and `crates/foxhound/sinks/`; the enforced direction reads "nothing outside `crates/foxhound/` but `xtask` depends on a crate inside it"; each Product's executable crate is a composition root; the test-only crate of turmoil's adapter has its reason; the Cargo features section is per Product; cargo-deny runs workspace-wide and on what each Product ships.
- **`AGENTS.md`.** The trap reads: a capability is a setting, never a Cargo feature, and nothing outside `crates/foxhound/` but `xtask` depends on a crate inside it.
- **Glossary.** *Size budget* is per Product. No new term: the values each executable composes are code, not domain language.
- **`TESTING.md`.** Collie's executable is tested against the generated table of Foxhound's names; the Politeness cases cover the navigation gate, and a handler's page past its lease sends nothing; turmoil's adapter is the one shared helper outside `goodfetch-test-support`; each executable crate tests its composition's handlers; Foxhound's tests, Simulation scenarios included, stay in Foxhound's crates.
- **ADR 0037's matrix.** SDK-03 cites this ADR. Areas 1, 3, 5, 6, 11 and 12 list it.
- **Areas.**
  - Area 1 (Repository, CI and release) builds the two checks, the per-Product builds and Size budgets, and `crates/foxhound/` in the workspace skeleton.
  - Area 3 (Engine runtime) builds the composition, the admission seam and `not_in_product`, and moves the modules that are Foxhound's out of `goodfetch-engine` (the per-domain queues and Politeness of `admission` and `politeness`, the crawl's work-item leases of `lease`, and `partition`). A piece that also serves one page, such as ADR 0053's per-proxy limits and their sharing, which Collie's exact capability set gives Collie, stays outside `crates/foxhound/`.
  - Area 5 (Site access) builds the page's value: the navigation gate in the request interception, the lease check before each command and in the raw CDP relay. Area 6 (Collection) sets it when a handler gets its page.
  - Area 11 (Client surfaces) builds the CLI's refusal of Foxhound's commands.
  - Area 12 (Console) builds the per-Product Console builds.
- **Left to other tickets.** Release jobs, signing, debug symbols, the PyPI quota per project and the test suites per Product are [Release, signing and testing of two products](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/459)'s (ADR 0094). What a Client of one Product does on an engine of the other at `hello` is #458's. Which executable a Browser host and Goodfetch Cloud's engines run, and which Console build Goodfetch Cloud serves, are [Which executables the Browser host and Goodfetch Cloud's engines run](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/498)'s (ADR 0096). [Apply the product-line decisions to the affected specs and tickets](https://github.com/goodfetch/goodfetch/issues/460) carries all of this into the specs and tickets.
