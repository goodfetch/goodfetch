# Proving ground

The website Goodfetch owns and generates (ADRs 0025, 0032 and 0063): every page comes from a seed, generated on request and never stored, and every task has an expected result derived from the same seed data, never from a tool's output. It depends on no Goodfetch crate but `goodfetch-test-support`, which `xtask dependency-direction` checks.

## `serve`

```sh
cargo run -p goodfetch-proving-ground -- serve --seed 158 --copy local --address 127.0.0.1:0 --network-profile none
```

The first output line is one JSON object: `address` (the bound address, port 0 replaced), `version` (the workspace version plus the commit, `0.0.0+<sha>`) and `seed` (an unsigned 64-bit integer). It serves until Ctrl-C or `SIGTERM`. Only the `local` copy and the `none` network profile exist yet; the online copy is #168's and the delayed and lossy profiles #160's.

## HTTP/1.1, HTTP/2 and TLS

The one address serves plain text and TLS: a connection whose first byte starts a TLS handshake gets TLS, any other plain text. Either way it speaks HTTP/2 when its first bytes are HTTP/2's preface (ALPN offers `h2` and `http/1.1`) and HTTP/1.1 otherwise.

TLS comes from a certificate authority the Proving ground makes from its seed (ADR 0059's one seeded authority): the same seed gives the same authority byte for byte, its keys derived from the seed and every signature RFC 6979's deterministic ECDSA on P-256. `GET /certificate-authority` serves it as PEM. A client trusts it explicitly (the engine's per-domain `trusted_cas`, ADR 0042, or a competitor's documented option); nothing turns verification off. Each host name below has its own leaf certificate, never a wildcard, dated from the clock and valid for 397 days, since some platforms refuse a longer-lived server certificate.

## Page families

Each family is served on its own host name, and each of its tasks on a host name of its own, `<ordinal>.<family host>`, which serves the same pages, so every request names its task. A client maps them to the bound address (the `resolve` setting, ADR 0059):

- `catalog.test`, and `1.catalog.test` to `3.catalog.test` for its tasks: the `catalog` family, ported from the lab's HTTP catalog. Listing pages `/catalog/1` to `/catalog/10` link ten products each and the next page (`rel="next"`); product pages `/products/P-0001` to `/products/P-0100` show each product's ID, name and price; `/products/P-NNNN/order` is the product's order form, which posts `product=P-NNNN` to `/orders` and orders it once.
- `rendered.test`, and `1.rendered.test` and `2.rendered.test` for its tasks: the `rendered` family, ADR 0025's AJAX loading, infinite scroll, shadow DOM and iframes. `/feed`'s script loads its 24 items from `/api/feed/1` to `/api/feed/4` by AJAX, the next page each time the end of the list comes into view. Each item page `/items/I-0001` to `/items/I-0024` loads its item from `/api/items/<id>`, puts the name in the open shadow root of its `item-card` element and the price in the page, and shows the description in a same-origin frame, `/items/<id>/details`. None of a Record's data is in a page's own HTML.
- `login.test`, and `1.login.test` and `2.login.test` for its tasks: the `login` family, ADR 0025's logins with MFA and logins that expire. `/login` takes a username and password; account 1 then asks for a TOTP code (RFC 6238: HMAC-SHA-1, 30-second steps, six digits) at `/mfa`, accepting the steps before and after the current one; account 2's login expires after three pages behind it. `/account` lists six orders, `/account/orders/O-01` to `O-06`; without a live session both send to `/login` (`303`). The accounts come from the seed, so each seed (and each Journey) has its own, and the task list names them.
- `schedule.test`, and `1.schedule.test` for its task: the `schedule` family, ADR 0025's content that changes on a known schedule. `/board` shows the revision of the current time step: it changes at every step but those that leave a remainder of 2 when divided by 3 (`R-0` at step 0, `R-1` at 1 and 2, `R-2` at 3, `R-3` at 4 and 5, and so on). The board at a step is a pure function of the seed and the step, never of the wall clock.
- `1.portal.test` to `13.portal.test`: the `portal` family, the lab's portal site, one variant per task, each Record an `id` and a `price`. Tasks 1 to 5 hold five pages of twenty reports (`/reports?page=N`) behind a login form (`/login`) whose CSRF token is tied to a `pre` cookie, with the account the task list gives; a session (`sid`) of task 2 serves four pages and is then answered with the login form and a `200`, one of task 3 serves three and is renewed by `POST /session/refresh` with its `rt` cookie, and a harness revokes every session between the two runs of task 5 (`POST /revoke-sessions` on the bound address). Each sign-in is an Effect, so a crawl that signs in more than it needs is judged by the site's own record. Tasks 6 to 11 hold one listing of a hundred products behind six renderings: server HTML (`/list?page=N`), a script shell (`/api/list`), partial hydration (eight in the HTML), stale HTML prices a script corrects (`/api/prices`), GraphQL behind "Load more" (`/catalog`, `POST /graphql`) and an infinite feed (`/feed`, `/api/feed?cursor=`). Task 12 is a crawler trap (`/shop`): every link carries a new `sid` and tracking parameters, categories have four sort orders and the events calendar pages on without end; a page is a pure function of the seed and its URL. Task 13 is a table of 1,500 orders (`/orders?page=N`, or `?after=<id>` through `rel="next"`) that, every third request, loses two rows already paged past and gains one on top; the oracle requires every row that lived through the crawl, once each, and tolerates the rows added or deleted.
- `<n>.actionability.test` and `<n>.navigation.test`: the hand-made `actionability` and `navigation` families, written by hand with their expected results in `pages/actionability.toml` and `pages/navigation.toml` (story 29), ported from the lab's actionability, primitive, form, navigation, frames and files fixtures. Each page's script posts the event that reaches one of its controls to `POST /effects` (`{"event", "target", "trusted", "details"}`, `trusted` the browser's `isTrusted`), recorded as an `event` Effect of the task; the oracle requires exactly the events listed: one trusted event of the right kind on the right control, none on a decoy, none twice. Tasks whose control never becomes actionable, stays covered, is ambiguous or is swapped for a destructive one under the pointer (`retarget`) expect a refusal. Cross-origin frames come from `<n>.navigation-frame.test`; the download (`navigation/7`) serves seeded bytes with a CRLF, a NUL and a `0xff` byte.

### Pages that break HTTP on purpose

These host names are served over TLS only, by a raw server that writes every byte itself (so a body can end before its `Content-Length`, an HTTP/2 stream can be reset after its headers, and so on); a plain-text request to one answers `421` with `tls_required`. Each family's tasks crawl a three-page shelf (`/catalog/1` to `/catalog/3`, two Records with `id` and `price` each, linked by `rel="next"`), the incident on its second page.

- `<n>.incidents.test`: the `incidents` family, the lab's HTTP incidents: `429` with `Retry-After: 1` once (task 1), `503` once (2), a body ending before its `Content-Length` (3), a dropped connection before a chunked body's last chunk (4), a redirect loop (5) and an answer after 8 seconds (6). Tasks 3 to 6 expect a reported failure; complete Records reported as a success are `late`, incomplete ones a false success.
- `<n>.access-policy.test`: the `access_policy` family, the lab's access-policy pages: the lab's robots.txt with `/private/` disallowed, which page 1 links into (task 1, judged by RFC 9309 from each request's `User-Agent`); robots.txt answering `503` (2); `Content-Signal: ai-train=no` for an AI-training crawl (3); `/.well-known/tdmrep.json` reserving `/catalog*` for text and data mining (4); `tdm-reservation: 1` (5), a `noai` robots meta tag (6), `402` (7), `403` (8) and a challenge served with `200` (9) on page 2. Tasks 2 to 9 expect a reported failure, page 1's Records tolerated where page 1 may be read.
- `<n>.identity.test`: the `identity` family, the lab's identity site. A request to any of these hosts carrying a Web Bot Auth signature is checked as it arrives with Cloudflare's `web-bot-auth`: tagged `web-bot-auth`, covering `@authority` and `Signature-Agent`, unexpired, and verified against the key directory its `Signature-Agent` names, fetched then from its `http://` URL (`/.well-known/http-message-signatures-directory` added to an origin), served as `application/http-message-signatures-directory+json`, each key's `kid` its RFC 7638 thumbprint and proven by the directory response's own signature (tag `http-message-signatures-directory`); a `data:` directory is read inline. Task 1 requires every request signed and verified, task 2 none signed.
- `<n>.content-coding.test`: the `content_coding` family (ADR 0119), one page `/page` per task: a plain body labelled `gzip` (1), an invalid gzip header (2), `gzip, br` (3), raw deflate (4), the unknown coding `utf-8` (5), gzip twice (6) and zstd twice (7), each labelled once. Tasks 3 to 5 expect the page's text, the others `navigation_failed` with `net::ERR_CONTENT_DECODING_FAILED`.
- `fetch-edge.test` (ADR 0118), with `h1.fetch-edge.test` (HTTP/1.1 alone in ALPN) and `h2.fetch-edge.test` (its certificate also naming `misdirected.fetch-edge.test`) serving the same pages. A page that fails once does so on its path's first request since `serve` started.
  - Redirects: `/redirect/loop`, `/redirect/cookie` (sets `hop=1` and returns to itself), `/redirect/301`, `302`, `303`, `307`, `308` and `300` to `/target`, `/redirect/no-location`, `/redirect/ftp`, `/redirect/cross-origin` (to `h1.fetch-edge.test`), `/redirect/hops/<n>`, and `/redirect/robots` to `/private/target`, which `/robots.txt` disallows.
  - Refresh stubs to `/target`: `/refresh/header` (`Refresh: 0`), `/refresh/meta/0`, `/refresh/meta/5`, `/refresh/meta/200`, `/refresh/noscript`, `/refresh/no-url`, `/refresh/self` and `/refresh/script`.
  - `Retry-After`: `/retry-after/<429|503|302>/<seconds|imf-fixdate|rfc850|asctime>` answers its status once with `Retry-After` of 2 s, as seconds or as an HTTP-date against a `Date` an hour behind the clock (the 302 to `/target`), then a page; `/retry-after/503/long` always asks for an hour.
  - Cut bodies: `/cut/content-length`, `/cut/chunked` (no last chunk), `/cut/reset` (HTTP/2: `RST_STREAM` after the headers), `/cut/connection` (the connection closes mid-body) and `/cut/gzip` (a whole transfer of a gzip stream cut halfway); `/close-delimited` is a whole body ended by the connection's close.
  - Requests never processed: `/refused` (`REFUSED_STREAM` once; `/refused/always` every time), `/http1-required` (`HTTP_1_1_REQUIRED` over HTTP/2), `/calm` (`ENHANCE_YOUR_CALM` once), `/reuse/close` and `/reuse/408` (a keep-alive connection closed, or answered `408`, as it is reused).
  - `goaway.fetch-edge.test` answers a connection's first request and sends a `GOAWAY` naming its stream to the next; `misdirected.fetch-edge.test` answers `421` on another host's connection; `ipv6.fetch-edge.test` is answered on IPv4 while the IPv6 loopback address on the same port takes no connection (its listener's queue is full: Linux drops the SYN, so an attempt times out; macOS resets it or queues it unanswered).

## Task list and oracle

On any other host name, such as the bound address itself:

- `GET /tasks` lists the tasks: `proving_ground` (`version`, `seed`) and `tasks`, each with `id` (`<family>/<ordinal>`), `family`, `kind` (`crawl`, `extract`, `submit`, `watch`, `act` or `download`), `start_url` (on the task's host name, with the bound port), `description` and `digest`. A task that signs in also has `account` (`username`, `password`, and `totp_secret` in unpadded base32 for an account with MFA); a watch task has `time_steps`, the count of steps from 0 it spans. The digest is the lowercase hex SHA-256 of `{"entry": …, "expected": …}`, every URL's port removed, in RFC 8785's canonical JSON, so it is the same on every port.
- `POST /oracle` judges a result: `{"task": "catalog/1", "status": "success" | "needs-review" | "failure", "records": [{"id": …, "name": …, "price": …}], "files": [{"name": …, "hex": …}]}`, `files` holding each downloaded file's name and bytes in hex. The answer names the `task`, its `outcome` (`verified`, `incomplete`, `false_success` or `failed`), the `tool_status` it recorded, the `missing` Record IDs, the `extra` Records, the `wrong` fields (`id`, `field`, `expected`, `submitted`), the `missing_effects` and `extra_effects`, the `missing_files`, `wrong_files` (`name`, `expected_sha256`, `submitted_sha256`) and `extra_files` when there are any, and `oracle_time_microseconds`. A correct result is `verified` whatever status the tool gave; only a result the tool calls a success is a false success. Effects are judged from the Proving ground's own record of what it carried out for the task, never from the tool's report, so an order placed twice is an extra Effect. A task whose expected result is a refusal is verified only when nothing was carried out and the tool reported a `failure`; a reported success is then a false success, unless it holds every Record the task lists, which only outwaiting its incident gives: data delivered late, `failed` with `late: true`. Where the refusal names an error, the submission's `error` (`{"code": …, "network_error": …}`) must be it, else `wrong_error` names both. The task's own observed requests are judged too: `forbidden_requests` lists those to a path the task must not request or its robots.txt disallows to their `User-Agent`, and `signature_faults` those whose Web Bot Auth signature is not as the task requires (`unsigned`, `refused` or `signed`). An unknown task answers `404` with `unknown_task`, an unreadable submission `400` with `malformed_submission`.
- `PUT /time-step` sets the time step, `{"step": 3}`, which starts at 0, and answers the step it set. Whoever runs a watch task sets each step in turn; a step that is not a whole number answers `400` with `malformed_time_step`.

## Observations

`GET /observations` serves what the Proving ground saw of every request to a host name it serves (its own address is not observed), for the oracle and the suites, never from a tool's report:

- `requests`, in arrival order, each with its `sequence`, `connection`, `task`, `host`, `protocol` (`http/1.1` or `h2`), `method`, `target`, `pseudo_header_order` (HTTP/2), `headers` in the order sent (names as sent), `cookies`, `message_signature` (the `Signature-Agent`, `Signature-Input` and `Signature` headers of RFC 9421, as Web Bot Auth sends them), `tls` (the connection's `ClientHello`: `sni`, `alpn`, `version`, `ciphers`, `extensions` in the order sent, `signature_algorithms`, `resumed`, its `ja4`, and `ja4_base` without the extensions a resumed handshake adds; ported from the lab's TLS fingerprint relay), `http2` (the client's first `settings` in the order sent, its first connection `window_update`, and the `stream`), `status`, `timing` (`arrived_microseconds` and `answered_microseconds` since `serve` started) and `bytes` (`received` and `sent`: the request's and its answer's messages as framed, HTTP/1.1's heads and bodies or the HTTP/2 frames of its stream); when there are any, `repeats` (the `sequence` of the latest earlier request of the same host, method and target), `next_request` (the next request to the same host: its `sequence` and `after_microseconds`, from this one's answer, or its arrival if it had none), `delivery` for the raw server's answers (`declared_length`, the `Content-Length` its head declared; `body_sent`; and `end`: `complete`, `connection_closed`, `stream_reset` with its `error_code`, or `go_away` with its `last_stream` and `error_code`), and the `message_signature`'s `verification` (`verified`, `key_id`, `error`);
- `connections`, each connection to a served host: its `connection`, `host`, the client's address `family` (`ipv4` or `ipv6`) and `accepted_microseconds`;
- `unrecorded_requests`, those past the first 100,000, which are counted in the bytes but not kept in detail;
- `effects`, each Effect the site carried out, with its `task`;
- `tasks` and `hosts`, the bytes `received` and `sent` per task and per host name: every byte of the connections to that host as they crossed the wire, TLS and HTTP/2's connection frames included, retries included. A connection's host is the server name its TLS handshake asked for, else the host of its first request. These are the benchmark's only source of bytes.
