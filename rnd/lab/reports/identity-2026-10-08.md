## What the session does

- **One profile per session.** `identified` or `standard`, set at opening; `switchProfile` to another profile raises `E_PROFILE_LOCKED`, and another profile requires a new session.
- **A single client.** The HTTP tier sends the headers that the session's browser actually sends. They are captured once, in order, on a local page owned by the session. Cookies follow escalation in both directions (`Storage.setCookies` and `Storage.getCookies` on the session's context).
- **Web Bot Auth** (`identified` profile). Every request from both tiers is signed:
  - RFC 9421 signatures in Ed25519, over `@authority` and `signature-agent`;
  - 60 s validity and a 64-byte nonce;
  - JWKS key directory served by the agent, the response being signed by each published key;
  - on the browser side, the signature is added through CDP interception (`Fetch.requestPaused`); a request that cannot be signed is not sent.

  The signer reproduces the signature from Appendix B.2.6 of RFC 9421, and its key identifiers reproduce the vector from Appendix A.3 of RFC 8037.
- **Site preferences**, loaded by the session's transport, hence under the same identity:
  - `robots.txt` according to RFC 9309, with `Crawl-delay`;
  - Content-Signal per purpose;
  - TDMRep (file, header and meta) for mining purposes;
  - meta `noai`.

  Then, 401, 402, 403 and challenge pages, even when served as 200, are typed stops. A 429 or a 503 with `Retry-After` is waited out within the attempt budget. The session journal records each decision with the hash of the `robots.txt` in force.

## The oracle

The test site goes through the lab's TLS relay: it records the JA4, HTTP/2 settings, HTTP version, headers and cookie of each request.

- **Signatures.** It verifies them on arrival with Cloudflare's `web-bot-auth` library and `http-message-sig`, not with the signer's code. It fetches the directory designated by `Signature-Agent`, checks the proof of possession of each key, then verifies the request signature against the keys published at that moment.
- **Identity.** Per session, it requires a single User-Agent, a single language, constant client hints, a single JA4 and a single HTTP/2 fingerprint. The session cookie must be present on every request following its issuance.
- **Favicon loads.** Chrome launches them itself, without client hints and sometimes before the cookie exists, as in any Chrome. The oracle counts them for User-Agent, TLS and HTTP/2, and leaves them out of the client hints and cookie continuity checks.
- **Expected outcome.** It is declared per variant, profile and purpose, independently of the policy engine.

- **Only the tier executed by the session's browser keeps an identity.** It performs a document load without script, in the same context as the pages opened afterwards. TLS, HTTP/2, headers and cookies are then those of the browser by construction.
- **Node** presents a different JA4 and stays on HTTP/1.1, whereas the browser speaks HTTP/2.
- **Impit in Chrome emulation** presents two fewer TLS extensions than the session's Chromium and an HTTP/2 header table of 4,096 instead of 65,536. In the `identified` profile, the browser sends no client hints (User-Agent overridden without metadata), but Impit adds those of its emulated Chrome 124.
- **The data remain correct.** Rotation and revocation pass in all tiers; only identity consistency fails.
- **Verdict.** `pnpm lab verdict D-identity` marks Impit and Node `blocked-by-contract` on `identity-coherence`, for both profiles, with the other three gates satisfied. Only verified cells enter the duration pairing: no speed estimate is produced for a tier that does not keep the identity, and the browser tier remains the reference option.

## Preferences and stop signals

The 132 cells of `policy-fixtures` are verified: 12 per variant, i.e. 2 profiles × 2 purposes × 3 tiers. These cells are judged on data and policy; the consistency violations of Impit and Node remain recorded there.

- **Expected refusals, without false success:**
  - path disallowed by `robots.txt` (the page is never requested);
  - `robots.txt` group specific to the `GoodfetchLab` token: the `identified` profile stops before any page, the `standard` profile follows the `*` group;
  - `Content-Signal: ai-train=no` stops the `ai-train` purpose before any page and lets `search` through;
  - TDMRep reservation by file or by header, and meta `noai`, which stop `ai-train` but not `search`;
  - 402, 403 and challenge served as 200.
- **Waits respected:**
  - `Crawl-delay: 1` spaces pages at least one second apart;
  - after a 429 with `Retry-After: 1`, the page is requested again after the delay.

## Isolation

The 18 cells are verified. Each session presents only its own cookie and does not see the local storage of the other, which runs in another context.

## Proxies

A session can go through its own local egress proxy, to which its browser and its HTTP tier point.
- This egress proxy opens each CONNECT tunnel through the session's sticky upstream proxy, designated by an identifier in the username, as with residential providers.
- A failed upstream is quarantined for a cooldown period, and the session switches to the least loaded healthy proxy, which consumes one attempt from the session's budget.
- With no healthy proxy within the budget, the session stops with `E_PROXY_EXHAUSTED`.

The browser context does not change: cookies, storage and TLS state remain those of the session.

The 18 cells are verified, without any violation. The judge relies on the tunnels recorded by the fixture's proxies:
- in normal operation, a single proxy per session;
- in case of failure, a single switch, after the failure, with no return to the abandoned proxy, with the same cookie and the same fingerprint;
- on exhaustion, the typed stop after two attempts, without false success.

The `proxy-continuity` gate of D-identity reads these variants. The proxies are only connected to the browser tier, the only one retained by the consistency gate.

## Defects found along the way

- **Request attribution.** The first version of the oracle grouped together the `robots.txt` requests of two simultaneous sessions, which have neither label nor cookie. It therefore reported an inconsistency between two distinct clients. These requests are now excluded when attribution is impossible.
- **Favicon.** It also faulted `/favicon.ico` for the absence of client hints and cookie. The runs made with this oracle are classified `diagnostic`: `identity-coherence-…2b3452` and `policy-fixtures-…00a1ff`, the latter interrupted.
- **JA4.** The lab's JA4 calculation kept the GREASE value that Chrome places in its signature algorithms and draws anew at each connection. Each reconnection therefore seemed to present a new client. A first version of this report wrongly concluded from this that two contexts of the same Chromium have two fingerprints. The defect is fixed; `identity-coherence-…deb797` and `session-isolation-…001cf2` are replaced by the runs above, with identical conclusions. A resumed handshake is compared without its `pre_shared_key` extension.

## Limitations

- The `stealth` profile is not prototyped.
- Proxies are only prototyped for the browser tier, and without a real cost per GB: bytes per proxy are recorded, but pricing remains out of scope.
- The browser signature covers the pages opened by the session, not out-of-process frames or workers.
- The directory is served over local HTTP; in production, the draft requires HTTPS.
- The durations come from a `local-dev` host and serve only for the internal verdict; the rerun in `local-controlled` is recorded in the ledger.
