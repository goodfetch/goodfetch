# Stagehand, CDP and latency — clarification as of October 7, 2026

**CDP is not, on its own, a reason to conclude that Stagehand is faster than Playwright.** Playwright's Chromium backend already uses a `CRConnection`, its CDP sessions and commands such as `Browser.getVersion` and `Target.setAutoAttach`. On top of that it adds its model of pages/frames, locators, waits and events. This concerns Chromium; its other browsers have their own backends. [Official Playwright code, CRBrowser](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/blob/main/packages/playwright-core/src/server/chromium/crBrowser.ts).

Two connections must be distinguished. `browserType.connect()` links a client to a Playwright server via the Playwright protocol. `connectOverCDP()` attaches Playwright to an already launched Chromium browser, via its CDP endpoint. The documentation describes **this external attachment mode** as less faithful than `connect()` and warns that launch arguments can compromise certain features. This caveat does not mean that Playwright's internal use of CDP is also a degraded mode. [Official connectOverCDP reference](https://playwright.dev/docs/api/class-browsertype#browser-type-connect-over-cdp).

The useful difference in Stagehand v4 is therefore its **own driver and the placement of the runtime in a Chrome extension**. Removing a Playwright dependency makes it possible to control the API surface and the implementation; it also means taking over the driver's guarantees. The runtime can decide and chain commands close to the browser, which is particularly interesting when the SDK and the browser are separated by a network. `experimentalBatch()` executes a callback in the service worker and avoids an SDK–browser round trip for each command. This is a placement/batching saving; the browser operations and any inference requested still exist. A Playwright execution placed close to the browser can also reduce this network part. [Stagehand reference, experimentalBatch](https://docs.stagehand.dev/v4/reference/stagehand).

The maintainers' post also motivates the placement by lifecycle/target races and the reuse of the already available and authenticated CDP socket, with no other exposed endpoint. It reports **a single 50-action journey**: batch 14.22 s versus Playwright 22.65 s, i.e. a throughput about 1.59× higher, with a 42.2 ms remote round trip. The authors explicitly ask that this be treated as one run, not as a general benchmark. Local proximity reduces the potential gain. [Maintainers' explanation](https://www.browserbase.com/blog/stagehand-v4).

**The trade-off is observable in the v4 contract.** The official migration indicates the absence of Playwright auto-waiting, `getBy*`, web-first assertions and route interception. Stagehand resolves the selector once; a prior explicit wait does not replace a per-action retry during a re-render. Some operations can thus finish earlier, but they do not necessarily perform the same checks. Comparing their raw duration may therefore compare different guarantees. [Official Playwright → Stagehand v4 migration](https://docs.stagehand.dev/v4/migrations/playwright).

**Measurable conclusion:** test the same browser, same placement, same action and same postconditions; separate SDK cost, transport, waits, capture and LLM. A vendor figure on launch, inference or batch does not prove an overall speedup on correct workflows. No benchmark was run in this note; no universal speed advantage is established.
