# Legal exposure of systematic cookie resets at scale

> **Not legal advice.** This is desk research by a non-lawyer, written to prepare a conversation with counsel. It maps where exposure appears to concentrate and lists questions to ask. It does not say any option is lawful or unlawful. No court has ruled on whether clearing cookies or opening a fresh browser session to get past a metered paywall is circumvention, unauthorized access or a breach of contract; the ratings below are inferred from the closest cases. A US, EU or French lawyer may read them differently.

**Research of October 12, 2026.** Ticket [#192](https://github.com/Jbmanllr/automan/issues/192), which is lawyer question 8 of [#154](https://github.com/Jbmanllr/automan/issues/154)'s note ([2026-10-12-paywall-consent-circumvention-legal.md](https://github.com/Jbmanllr/automan/blob/research/paywall-consent-circumvention/rnd/research/2026-10-12-paywall-consent-circumvention-legal.md), "the #154 note" below). Feeds [ADR 0052](../../docs/adr/0052-a-wall-allowance-lets-a-person-approve-reading-past-walls-by-generic-means.md). Cited, not repeated: the #154 note's takedown record (Bypass Paywalls Clean, 12ft.io, archive.today), its competitor table, its texts of §1201, InfoSoc Art. 6 and CPI L.331-5, and its reading of *Ziff Davis*, *MDY*, *Healthcare Advocates* and *Register.com*; the [#65 note](https://github.com/Jbmanllr/automan/blob/research/stealth-legal-exposure/rnd/research/2026-10-10-stealth-legal-exposure.md)'s reading of *Google v. SerpApi*, *Van Buren*, *hiQ*, *3Taps*, *Meta* and *X Corp. v. Bright Data*, *Ryanair*, *Bluetouff*, CPI L.335-3-1 and R.335-3, and Code pénal 323-1. **One correction to the #154 note:** it found no US ruling on a news paywall; *Emmerich Newspapers v. Particle Media* (below) is one, though it turns on access already granted, not on a meter.

**Source tags:** **[P]** primary source read this session (statute, judgment, docket, official filing, takedown notice, regulator text, standard, first-party document). **[P\*]** primary source cited but not read in full this session: read in part, through a mirror, through an earlier note, or as quoted by another court. **[C]** credible secondary source. **[V]** vendor or party self-description. **[U]** unverified. **[I]** my inference.

**What ADR 0052 does.** On a domain a person lists in `wall_domains`, when a paywall leaves the served page without its main content, the engine retires the Session and fetches the page once more in a new one, with a fresh cookie jar and storage. It never does this on a logged-in or `identified` Session, does it at most once per page, never counts it as a block for escalation, and never does it for a consent or ad-blocker wall. The Audit log records each widening of `wall_domains` with the warning shown, and one entry per run and domain; Provenance records `new_session` per page. Goodfetch's code names no vendor and no "meter reset".

## Answer

Scale and placement change the exposure more than the act does. A one-off reset stays Low everywhere: no ruling, complaint or takedown was found that turns on clearing cookies, private browsing or resetting a meter; the CJEU held in July 2026 that a measure which "depends entirely on the internet user's willingness" is not effective; the cookie standard lets the browser drop a site's state at any time; and Orin Kerr's CFAA analysis treats cookie limits as "speed bumps rather than barriers". Done systematically by an automated client, the same act becomes Medium for the user in the US, the EU and France, and High for Goodfetch Cloud in all three. *Reddit v. SerpApi* refuses to excuse circumvention of a measure against "bulk access" because each item is open to an individual, and treats shifting IP addresses to avoid repeated-request counting as circumvention; *CoxCom* held that a filter keeping pay-per-view purchases from being billed let subscribers "avoid" or "bypass" the measure, which is what a reset does to a meter's count; US statutory damages run per act, and one court multiplied them by 11,786 acts; German unfair-competition law, French *parasitisme* and the database right's "repeated and systematic" clause all turn on repetition and commerce; and a hosted service is both the direct actor and a "service" provider. ADR 0052's safeguards help where they keep the reset away from logins, loops and escalation, but its design raises the public framework from the #154 note's Low to Medium, because a step that fires only when a paywall withholds content is a meter reset by function, and because the fresh Session can arrive with a new IP address and fingerprint. Four changes keep it close to a plain cookie reset: reset only cookies and storage, keeping the proxy exit and Identity profile; make the reset lockable on its own and lock it in Goodfetch Cloud; stop on a block or a recorded objection; keep the docs factual. No rating for the public framework or a user reaches High, so under #192's rule the reset stays; but ADR 0052's sentence that clearing cookies "rates Low too, as long as it is not presented as a meter reset" does not hold for a paywall-triggered reset and should be corrected.

## Ratings [I]

US / EU / FR. L = Low, M = Medium, H = High. Each cell rates the exposure of the party in that column:

- **A, public framework:** Goodfetch as publisher of the `MIT OR Apache-2.0` code.
- **U, a user running it:** the person or company whose run resets.
- **P, a user's own Plugin:** the Plugin's author and user, when the reset lives in their Plugin rather than in Goodfetch's code. Goodfetch's own exposure for such a Plugin stays L in every row: it ships only generic hooks (§1201(c)(3)), and ADR 0052 keeps such Plugins out of the index.
- **B, Goodfetch Cloud:** Goodfetch as the operator that fetches.

"Systematic" means a reset on every page a meter withholds, across a site and over time.

| Scenario | A public framework | U a user running it | P a user's own Plugin | B Goodfetch Cloud |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **One-off reset** (one page, one fresh state) | **L / L / L** | **L / L / L** | **L / L / L** | **L / L / L** |
| **Systematic resets by a user's run** (self-hosted) | **L / L / L**¹ | **M / M / M** | **M / M / M**² | — |
| **Systematic resets in Goodfetch Cloud** | — | **M / M / M**³ | **M / M / M**³ | **H / H / H** |
| **ADR 0052's design as specified** | **M / M / M** | **M / M / M**⁴ | **L / L / L**⁵ | **H / H / H**⁶ |

1. Done through generic means the user sets up, such as a Hook that opens a new Session or a call that clears cookies: the #154 note's "generic" profile.
2. **H / M / M** for the author if they publish the Plugin presented as a meter reset, which is the Bypass Paywalls Clean profile.
3. The customer who sets the run. *Reddit* treats a customer alleged to set "operational inputs that determine when, how, and at what scale circumvention will occur" as a direct circumventor (at 48) [P]; in France, using such a service is R.335-3's petty offense (#65 note).
4. **H / M / M** when the fresh Session also changes IP address or fingerprint, as it can today (see "ADR 0052" below): that is the #154 note's identity disguise.
5. ADR 0052 puts the reset in Goodfetch's code, not in a Plugin, so this cell is Goodfetch's exposure for users' Plugins under its index rule. A Plugin author who resets beyond ADR 0052 is in row 2.
6. If Goodfetch Cloud offers the allowance; ADR 0052 leaves that to the cloud map. **L** wherever Operator policy locks the reset off.

**What moves a cell.**
- **One-off to systematic** moves U from L to M because the remedies, the evidence of purpose and the non-copyright theories all scale (below). The definition of circumvention does not change, but *Reddit*'s "bulk access" passage weakens the main defense. In the EU column the Medium rests mostly on Germany: § 108b UrhG makes non-private circumvention a crime if the meter counts as effective, and a media-monitoring firm lost over a cookie-metered newspaper's content (OLG München, below). Elsewhere in the EU, *Anne Frank Fonds* ¶54 points to Low.
- **Self-hosted to Cloud** moves the actor from the user to Goodfetch, and from M to H. The Cloud is a commercial direct circumventor and a "service" provider. In Germany it is the party that gives others "unberechtigten Zugang zu einer entgeltlich angebotenen Leistung", and in France the one that profits from the publisher's investment.
- **Generic hook to ADR 0052** moves A from L to M because the trigger is a paywall that withholds content. That is the design prong of trafficking in the US and France, and in Germany a product that undermines a protective precaution guarding a paid offer.

## Analysis per legal theory

### Circumvention

**Is a cookie or localStorage meter an "effective" measure?**

*What a meter is.* A metered paywall counts a visitor's free articles in state the site asks the browser to keep: a cookie, localStorage, or a count on the server keyed to an identifier the browser sends back. A visitor without state gets the free allowance. The cookie standard tells servers to "gracefully degrade if the user agent fails to return one or more cookies ... because the user agent might evict any cookie at any time on orders from the user" ([RFC 6265 §6.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6265#section-6.1)), and browsers "SHOULD provide users with a mechanism for disabling cookies" (§7.2) [P]. When Google closed Chrome's incognito-detection loophole in July 2019, it called meters inherently porous, noted that private browsing is one way people reset the count, and told publishers to lower the free allowance, require free registration or harden the paywall rather than detect private browsing ([Google, July 18, 2019](https://blog.google/outreach-initiatives/google-news-initiative/protecting-private-browsing-chrome/)) [P]. The CNIL lists trackers that let paying sites "limiter l'accès gratuit à un échantillon de contenu" among those exempt from consent ([Délibération 2020-091, ¶49](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/cnil/files/atoms/files/lignes_directrices_de_la_cnil_sur_les_cookies_et_autres_traceurs.pdf)) [P]. That makes the meter a recognized, legitimate mechanism; it says nothing about whether the meter is protected against circumvention.

*United States, §1201(a)(3)(B).* A measure "effectively controls access" if it "in the ordinary course of its operation, requires the application of information, or a process or a treatment, with the authority of the copyright owner, to gain access" ([§1201](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1201)) [P].
- **Against.**
  - A meter requires nothing of a visitor without state. It restricts only a browser that sends back what the site asked it to keep, which the browser may stop doing. That is *Lexmark*'s house whose "front door does not contain a lock" (387 F.3d at 547, as quoted in *Reddit* at 39) [P\*], and it echoes *Healthcare Advocates* and *Ziff Davis* (#154 note).
  - A visitor who uses the mechanism as designed does not circumvent it: "the authorized use of a technological measure does not constitute circumvention" ([*Egilman v. Keller & Heckman*, 401 F. Supp. 2d 105, 113 (D.D.C. 2005)](https://static.case.law/f-supp-2d/401/html/0105-01.html)) [P\*]; the statute "is focused on the method of entry" ([*Dish Network v. World Cable*, 893 F. Supp. 2d 452, 466 (E.D.N.Y. 2012)](https://static.case.law/f-supp-2d/893/html/0452-01.html)) [P\*]. A browser with no state is let in by the meter working as designed. [I]
  - The only US paywall ruling found went against the publisher. *Emmerich Newspapers v. Particle Media* gave the aggregator summary judgment on circumvention: the publisher had "hard paywalls", but its complaint was about "use of its content, not its unauthorized access to the content", since the full text was in the HTML the crawler fetched ([S.D. Miss. No. 3:23-cv-26, ECF 317 at 44–46 (July 29, 2025)](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mssd.117274/gov.uscourts.mssd.117274.317.0.pdf)) [P]. On appeal the Fifth Circuit remanded on other grounds and called §1201 "a viable pathway to hold accountable parties that do engage in demonstratable circumvention" ([No. 25-60550, slip op. at 36–37 (Aug. 27, 2026)](https://storage.courtlistener.com/pdf/2026/08/27/emmerich_nwspr_v._particle_media.pdf)) [P].
  - The EFF's Naomi Gilens argued in 2020 that if a site lets visitors in without cookies, nothing effectively controls access ([The Register, July 21, 2020](https://www.theregister.com/2020/07/21/cookie_clearing_chrome_extension_dmca/)) [C].
- **For.**
  - After the free allowance, gaining access in the ordinary course requires a subscriber's credentials. That is how the News/Media Alliance framed its members' metered paywalls in its 2024 GitHub notice: password-protected sign-in, for all content or "after a user has accessed a certain number of articles" ([notice](https://github.com/github/dmca/blob/master/2024/08/2024-08-09-news-media-alliance.md)) [P]. Troupson's NYU note reaches the same view on the exact facts of a reader who deletes his cookies and reloads ([90 N.Y.U. L. Rev. 325, 326–27, 351 (2015)](https://www.nyulawreview.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/NYULawReview-90-1-Troupson.pdf)) [C].
  - The closest analogy to a meter is a usage counter, and *CoxCom* held that a cable filter that stopped the pay-per-view "delivery and billing system" from registering purchases let subscribers "'avoid' or 'bypass' that technological measure" ([*CoxCom v. Chaffee*, 536 F.3d 101, 110 (1st Cir. 2008)](https://static.case.law/f3d/536/html/0101-01.html)) [P\*]. A reset keeps the meter's counter from counting. [I]
  - Courts read "effectively" loosely: a measure need not be "strong or circumvention-proof", only "provide some degree of control", and whether a work "may be viewed by the public is immaterial" ([*Cordova v. Huneault*, N.D. Cal. No. 25-cv-04685-VKD, ECF 65 at 14 (Jan. 23, 2026)](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.450549/gov.uscourts.cand.450549.65.0_1.pdf), quoting *MDY* n.17) [P].
  - *Reddit v. SerpApi* is the strongest authority against the open-front-door argument at scale. SearchGuard does not stop being an access control because its restriction "depends on the character of the user", and nothing in the statute "exempts from liability persons who circumvent TPMs aimed at protecting copyrightable material against bulk access merely because that material is accessible to an authorized individual" ([S.D.N.Y. No. 1:25-cv-08736, ECF 104 at 39–40 (July 31, 2026)](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.651592/gov.uscourts.nysd.651592.104.0.pdf)) [P]. It distinguished *Lexmark* because SearchGuard does not leave the content "freely readable" (at 39).
- **Reading them together [I].** A meter is a measure against one client reading many articles. The open front door argues well for a reader who resets once and badly for a run that resets on every page. A meter whose article is already in the HTML is *Emmerich*'s case and the #154 note's L1; a server-side meter that withholds the body is the case a reset is for, and *CoxCom* and *Reddit* are its closest authorities.

*European Union.* InfoSoc Art. 6(3) deems a measure effective where use is "controlled by the rightholders through application of an access control or protection process ... which achieves the protection objective" ([Directive 2001/29, Art. 6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2001/29/article/6)) [P]. The CJEU's *Anne Frank Fonds* judgment is the closest analogy, and it cuts both ways ([C-788/24, July 9, 2026, provisional text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62024CJ0788)) [P]:
- effectiveness "does not have to be absolute" and is judged by proportionality (¶46); that a measure can be circumvented, there by a VPN, "cannot, in itself and in all circumstances, be a decisive factor" (¶51);
- but a measure that "ultimately depends entirely on the internet user's willingness or unwillingness" is not effective (¶54, about a self-declaration of the user's country);
- a "subscription or login protected by a username and password" is the kind of measure that clearly counts (¶53).

A client-side meter, and a server-side count keyed to an identifier the browser may withhold, both depend on the browser cooperating, while the login the meter leads to is the measure ¶53 has in mind. On that reading, resetting the meter does not touch an effective measure in the EU, and getting past the login would. [I] *VG Bild-Kunst* adds that a rightholder cannot limit consent to online use "by means other than effective technological measures" (C-392/19, ¶46) [P\*]. That makes a meter's effectiveness decisive also for whether content read past it reaches a "new public".

*Germany.*
- § 95a(1) UrhG bars circumventing an effective measure where the actor knows or should know it is done to gain access, and § 95a(2) requires a mechanism that "die Erreichung des Schutzziels sicherstellen" ([§ 95a](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__95a.html)) [P]. § 108b(1) makes circumvention to gain access a crime unless done "ausschließlich zum eigenen privaten Gebrauch", with up to three years when commercial (§ 108b(3)) ([§ 108b](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__108b.html)) [P]. A commercial scraper is outside the private-use exception, so the German exposure turns on whether a meter is "wirksam".
- The BGH's *Session-ID* decision treated session identifiers that limited deep links as a protective measure for § 19a purposes without asking whether they were effective under § 95a, although the court below had found them "problemlos überwindbar" ([I ZR 39/08, Apr. 29, 2010, Leitsatz a, Rn. 9, 30, 33](https://www.bundesgerichtshof.de/SharedDocs/Entscheidungen/DE/Zivilsenate/I_ZS/2008/I_ZR__39-08.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=1)) [P\*].
- OLG München applied that logic to a newspaper whose meter counted nine free articles a day through cookies, against a media-monitoring firm that reproduced the articles ([29 U 953/16, July 14, 2016](https://www.haufe.de/id/entscheidungen/olg-muenchen-urteil-vom-14072016-29-u-95316-HI9728478.html)) [C]. *VG Bild-Kunst* (2021) probably displaces the lower *Session-ID* threshold. [I]

*France.* CPI L.331-5 deems a measure effective when use "est contrôlée par les titulaires de droits grâce à l'application d'un code d'accès, d'un procédé de protection tel que le cryptage, le brouillage ...", and names press publications (#154 and #65 notes) [P\*]. A count kept in a cookie is neither an access code nor a transformation of the work. The argument for it is that "code d'accès" renders the Directive's "access control", which the CJEU interprets for the whole Union; *Anne Frank Fonds* ¶54 now argues the other way. [I]

**Does clearing one's own state "avoid or bypass" it?**
- **US.** If the meter is an access control, clearing the state to be counted afresh fits "avoid" or "bypass" (§1201(a)(3)(A)) as *Reddit* read those words: SerpApi's "shifting IP addresses to avoid multiple requests from the same address and sending fake user-agent strings" avoided SearchGuard (at 44–45), and *Google v. SerpApi* had found that SerpApi plausibly bypassed it or impaired "its ability to detect and block" (as quoted in *Reddit* at 45) [P]. A fresh cookie jar per page avoids multiple requests from the same cookie. [I] *Ticketmaster v. Prestige* recited "cookie trading" among the ways ticket bots circumvented Ticketmaster's countermeasures, in a complaint whose DMCA claim survived on the CAPTCHA ([315 F. Supp. 3d 1147, 1155 (C.D. Cal. 2018)](https://static.case.law/f-supp-3d/315/html/1147-01.html)) [P\*].
- **The act must be affirmative.** Circumvention means an action that "disables or voids" the measure (*Reddit* at 47, quoting *LivePerson* and *Dish Network*; *Ziff Davis*, #154 note) [P]. A browser that never stores cookies is closer to passive non-compliance; a step that detects the paywall and refetches without the counted state is an affirmative response to the measure. [I]
- **§1201(i), the privacy carve-out, does not help.** It covers only an act that "has no other effect on the ability of any person to gain access to any work", done "solely for the purpose of preventing the collection" of personal data ([§1201(i)(1)(C)–(D)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1201)) [P].
- **EU and France: knowledge.** Art. 6(1) reaches circumvention done "in the knowledge, or with reasonable grounds to know"; L.335-3-1 requires "sciemment" (#65 note) [P]. ADR 0052's warning at each widening makes a user's later resets knowing. [I]
- **France: which offense.** L.335-3-1 I punishes defeating a measure by a "personal intervention" made without a means of the kind in II (€3,750). II punishes making available, or "fournissant un service" with, means "conçus ou spécialement adaptés" (6 months and €30,000; five times the fine and a possible activity ban for a company). R.335-3 punishes using such a means (up to €750) (#65 note) [P\*]. The more ADR 0052's reset reads as "spécialement adapté" to paywalls, the more Goodfetch moves into II and the user down to R.335-3. [I]

**Do scale and automation change it?** Not the definition, but almost everything around it.
- **The defense weakens.** *Reddit*'s "bulk access" passage answers the open-front-door argument for an automated client, not for a reader (above). [I]
- **Remedies multiply.** US statutory damages run from $200 to $2,500 "per act of circumvention, device, product, component, offer, or performance of service" (§1203(c)(3)(A)). A court may cut them only for a violator who "was not aware and had no reason to believe" it was violating the statute (§1203(c)(5)(A)), which a person-approved warning rules out ([§1203](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1203)) [P]. One court counted each end user's access as an act and awarded "11,786 acts of circumvention" times $200, or $2,357,200, on a default judgment ([*Stockwire Research Group v. Lebed*, 577 F. Supp. 2d 1262, 1267–68 (D. Utah 2008)](https://static.case.law/f-supp-2d/577/html/1262-01.html)) [P\*]. If each per-page reset is an act, exposure grows with the page count. [I] Criminal liability needs wilfulness "for purposes of commercial advantage or private financial gain" (§1204(a)), which a paid service fits ([§1204](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1204)) [P].
- **Purpose becomes evident.** A run that resets on every withheld page documents its purpose in its own records: ADR 0052's Provenance logs `new_session` per page. [I]
- **Detection.** A meter reset by hand is invisible; one done on every page of a site from one IP address is a pattern the publisher's logs show. [I]

**The component: trafficking.** §1201(a)(2) reaches "any technology, product, service, device, component, or part thereof" that is "primarily designed or produced" to circumvent, has "only limited commercially significant purpose or use" otherwise, or is "marketed" for it. §1201(c)(3), which frees products from having to respond to any measure, applies only "so long as such part or component ... does not otherwise fall within" (a)(2) [P]. InfoSoc Art. 6(2)(c) and § 95a(3) Nr. 3 UrhG add "adapted" ("angepasst") to "primarily designed" [P]; L.335-3-1 II says "spécialement adaptés" [P\*].
- A general call that clears cookies or opens a Session is primarily designed for nothing in particular.
- A step that fires only when a paywall withholds the main content, and refetches the page without the counted state, is primarily designed, or at least adapted, to get past a meter.
- Naming decides the marketing prong; function decides the design prong. GitHub's §1201 guide asks the complainant to explain "how the accused project is designed to circumvent" the measure ([guide](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/content-removal-policies/guide-to-submitting-a-dmca-takedown-notice)) [P], and ADR 0052, public in the repository, states the trigger. [I]

### Unauthorized access

**United States: CFAA and Cal. Penal Code §502.**
- **Public pages.** A metered article is served to anyone who arrives without state. Under *Van Buren*'s gates-up-or-down test and *hiQ II* for public pages (#65 note), a fresh Session that the site lets in as a new visitor passes a gate that is up. [I] Kerr argues the point directly for cookie meters: browsers let users accept, reject or clear cookies as often as they like, so access after clearing them remains authorized, and cookie limits are "speed bumps rather than barriers" ([Norms of Computer Trespass, 116 Colum. L. Rev. 1143, 1166–67 (2016)](https://www.columbialawreview.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Orin-S.-Kerr.pdf)) [C]. His parallel view that IP blocks are also speed bumps lost in *3Taps* (at 1167–68).
- **Terms are not gates.** *Sandvig v. Barr* held that terms of service "do not constitute 'permission requirements'", and that liability arises "only when the user bypasses an authenticating permission requirement" ([D.D.C. Mar. 27, 2020, slip op. at 20, 22](https://storage.courtlistener.com/pdf/2020/03/27/sandvig_v._sessions.pdf)) [P\*]. That case concerned researchers creating fictitious accounts, which is the closest US analogy to presenting a fresh identity.
- **Standard browser functions.** Under §502, Google's defeat of Safari's cookie blocking was not access "without permission", because Google "exploited a standard Safari browser function" ([*In re Google Inc. Cookie Placement*, 988 F. Supp. 2d 434, 449 (D. Del. 2013)](https://static.case.law/f-supp-2d/988/html/0434-01.html)) [P\*]. The district court in *Power Ventures* framed §502 as reaching access that circumvents "technical or code-based barriers" (N.D. Cal. July 20, 2010, ECF 89 at 18) [P\*]; clearing state with an ordinary browser function is a weak candidate. [I]
- **After the site says no.** The Ninth Circuit held that once Facebook revoked permission, "technological gamesmanship" such as getting around IP blocks "will not excuse liability" ([*Facebook v. Power Ventures*, 844 F.3d 1058, 1067–68 (9th Cir. 2016)](https://static.case.law/f3d/844/html/1058-01.html)) [P\*]; *3Taps* held the same (#65 note). In *US v. Lowson*, a CFAA indictment of ticket buyers who used thousands of IP addresses "to create the illusion" of separate customers survived a motion to dismiss (D.N.J. Oct. 12, 2010, ECF 53 at 4, 10) [P\*]. A systematic reset program that keeps running after a notice or a block is that pattern. [I]
- **Logged-in accounts.** In *Amazon v. Perplexity*, the district court enjoined Comet's use of users' password-protected Amazon accounts after a cease-and-desist ([N.D. Cal. No. 3:25-cv-09514-MMC, ECF 81 at 3–4 (Mar. 9, 2026)](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.459191/gov.uscourts.cand.459191.81.0.pdf)) [P]. The Ninth Circuit vacated: the AI agent "is a tool, not a person for statutory purposes", and "It is the user who 'accesses' Amazon's computers" ([No. 26-1444, slip op. at 15 (Aug. 4, 2026)](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/08/04/26-1444.pdf)) [P]; it left open whether Perplexity disguised the agent as Chrome (n.1). That reasoning helps a framework whose user runs it, not a cloud that runs its own Sessions, and the fight over logged-in accounts is why ADR 0052's "never on a logged-in Session" matters. [I]
- **Loss.** A civil CFAA claim needs loss of at least $5,000 in a year (18 U.S.C. §1030(g), (c)(4)(A)(i)(I)) ([§1030](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1030)) [P]. One reset never reaches it; a service refetching every withheld page of a large site might, through the publisher's response costs. *Ryanair v. Booking* failed on proof of loss (#65 note). [I]

**France: Code pénal 323-1.** *Bluetouff* punished staying in a system after noticing a login page (#65 note) [P\*]. The Paris court of appeal's *Kitetoa* decision held that reaching unprotected data "avec un simple navigateur" is not fraudulent access (CA Paris, Oct. 30, 2002, n° 02/04867) [C]. A meter that any browser resets is closer to *Kitetoa*; continuing after a written objection is closer to *Bluetouff*'s *maintien*. [I]

**Germany: § 202a StGB.** It requires data "gegen unberechtigten Zugang besonders gesichert" and access "unter Überwindung der Zugangssicherung" ([§ 202a](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__202a.html)) [P]. The BGH requires a safeguard that excludes access or at least makes it "nicht unerheblich" harder. Mere circumvention of "organisatorischen Maßnahmen oder Registrierungspflichten" falls outside it, as do cases where breaking the protection "für jedermann ohne weiteres möglich ist" ([5 StR 614/19, May 13, 2020, Rn. 19, 23, 24](https://www.bundesgerichtshof.de/SharedDocs/Entscheidungen/DE/Strafsenate/5_StS/2019/5_StR_614-19.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=1)) [P]. A cookie counter that any browser resets fits the last category. [I]

### Contract

- **The terms exist.**
  - The New York Times' terms (effective Jan. 20, 2026) bar tools or processes designed to get around its access controls or "use limits", and automated scraping; assent is by use (§§1.1, 1.3, 4.1(2), 4.1(4)) [P].
  - Dow Jones' terms (June 30, 2026) bar automated access, volumes beyond personal consumption and circumventing use restrictions, and require truthful headers, cookies included (9.4.1 to 9.4.3) [P].
  - Le Figaro's terms bind on browsing and bar automated extraction, with no clause on cookies or the paywall [P]. Der Spiegel's (v2.3.0, Apr. 11, 2025) bar crawlers and content-blocking tools, also with no paywall clause [P].
  - The Washington Post's and Le Monde's terms could not be read: each served a bot challenge, which was not bypassed [U].
- **United States: assent.** A browsewrap binds a user put on "inquiry notice", and a conspicuous hyperlink "without more" is not enough ([*Nguyen v. Barnes & Noble*, 763 F.3d 1171, 1177, 1179 (9th Cir. 2014)](https://static.case.law/f3d/763/html/1171-01.html)) [P\*]; *Berman v. Freedom Financial* (9th Cir. 2022) and *Meyer v. Uber* ([868 F.3d 66, 74–75 (2d Cir. 2017)](https://static.case.law/f3d/868/html/0066-01.html)) apply the same notice test [P\*]. Actual notice is different. *Nguyen* notes that courts enforce browsewraps where the user had it (at 1176), and Verio was bound because each of its daily queries gave it notice of the terms (*Register.com*, #154 note) [P\*]. A run that resets on every page has met the meter, and the terms behind it, many times. [I]
- **United States: limits.** A logged-off scraper was not bound by Meta's terms (#65 note) [P\*]. In *X Corp. v. Bright Data*, contract claims aimed at copying public content were preempted by the Copyright Act, but claims based on access survived amendment when X pleaded scraping "purpose-built to exceed rate limits at monumental scale" by a scraper that "came right back disguised to evade X's anomaly detection" ([ECF 156 at 9–12, 17 (N.D. Cal. Nov. 26, 2024)](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.415869/gov.uscourts.cand.415869.156.0_1.pdf)) [P]. Terms that limit access, such as a meter or a ban on automation, survive preemption better than terms that forbid copying. [I]
- **France.** Code civil art. 1119: general conditions bind the other party only if "portées à la connaissance de celle-ci et si elle les a acceptées" [P\*, WIPO Lex consolidation of Aug. 13, 2025]. Terms reached only through a footer link bind a robot weakly. No French ruling on browsewrap terms and robots was found [U].
- **EU and Germany.** Where neither copyright nor the *sui generis* right protects a database, its maker may restrict its use by contract (*Ryanair v. PR Aviation*, #65 note) [P\*]. The BGH held that ignoring terms ticked in a checkbox is "primär vertragsrechtlich" and not unfair in itself ([*Flugvermittlung*, I ZR 224/12, Rn. 35, 38](https://www.bundesgerichtshof.de/SharedDocs/Entscheidungen/DE/Zivilsenate/I_ZS/2012/I_ZR_224-12.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=1)) [P].
- **ADR 0052's "never on a logged-in Session"** removes the strongest contract case. A subscriber accepted the terms by clickwrap, and resetting in that Session would both breach them and log the account out. *hiQ* lost on contract over fake accounts used for scraping (#65 note). [I]

### Database right

- Directive 96/9 Art. 7(5) bars "repeated and systematic extraction and/or re-utilization of insubstantial parts" that conflict with normal exploitation ([Directive 96/9](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:31996L0009)) [P]. CPI L.342-2 is the French version, for acts that "excèdent manifestement les conditions d'utilisation normale de la base de données" [P\*], and § 87b(1) UrhG the German one [P]. This is the one clause written for systematic, piecemeal extraction, and a reset on every withheld page is that. [I]
- The obstacle is the investment. Only investment in "obtaining" existing materials counts, not investment in creating them (*British Horseracing Board*, C-203/02, ¶31) [P], and the test is the "risk that that investment may not be redeemed" (*CV-Online*, C-762/19, ¶¶44, 46–47) [P]. Writing journalism is creation, so a news site's article archive is a weak database claim, and no ruling treating a news site's articles as a protected database was found [U]. Classified-ads sites succeed: the Cour de cassation upheld Leboncoin's claim against a scraper and counted its advertising spend to attract listings as "obtaining" (Cass. 1re civ., Oct. 5, 2022, n° 21-16.307, ¶¶11–12, 19) [P\*, via Juricaf].
- A lawful user may take insubstantial parts (Art. 8(1); L.342-3 1°) [P\*]. Whether a reader who resets the meter is a "lawful user" is open. [I]

### Unfair competition and trespass

- **Germany, § 4 Nr. 4 UWG.** The BGH says offering goods or services can be unfair indirect interference when they are suited to give third parties "einen unberechtigten Zugang zu einer entgeltlich angebotenen Leistung", and that supplying a product is regularly unfair "wenn dabei eine Schutzvorkehrung unterlaufen wird" ([*Werbeblocker II*, I ZR 154/16, Apr. 19, 2018, Rn. 31](https://www.bundesgerichtshof.de/SharedDocs/Entscheidungen/DE/Zivilsenate/I_ZS/2016/I_ZR_154-16.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=1)) [P]. Adblock Plus escaped because no anti-adblock measure was found to be circumvented (Rn. 32). *Flugvermittlung* adds that "Überwindung einer technischen Schutzvorrichtung" can make scraping unfair (Rn. 37) [P].
  - Unlike § 95a, this test asks for a protective precaution, not an *effective* measure, and a meter is a precaution that guards a paid offer. [I]
  - It needs a competitive relationship. The BGH found one between free, ad-funded editorial content and ad-blocking software, because both are aimed at the same readers (Rn. 18) [P]. A framework or a cloud that hands those readers the paid content fits that reasoning; the cloud fits it better. [I]
- **France, *parasitisme*.** It is a fault under Code civil art. 1240: to "se placer dans le sillage d'un autre afin de tirer indûment profit" of another's efforts and investments, with the claimant identifying a "valeur économique individualisée" (Cass. com., Mar. 5, 2025, n° 23-21.157, ¶¶5–6) [P\*, via Juricaf]. The action is available without any competitive relationship (Cass. com., Mar. 18, 2026, n° 24-17.016, ¶25) [P\*, via Juricaf]. A service that systematically reads metered journalism for paying customers fits the definition better than any other theory here; one-off or private use does not. No press publisher's *parasitisme* ruling against an aggregator or scraper was found [U].
- **United States: trespass to chattels and unfair competition.** In *X Corp. v. Bright Data*, the court let X re-plead trespass to chattels and the "unlawful" and "fraudulent" prongs of California's UCL once X alleged server impairment and knowing evasion at scale (ECF 156 at 5–13) [P]. The scale and evasion of a systematic reset program are the facts that revived those claims. [I]

### Press publishers' right

DSM Art. 15 gives press publishers rights over "online use of their press publications by information society service providers", excludes "private or non-commercial uses of press publications by individual users", and applies InfoSoc Art. 6 to that right (Art. 15(1), (3)) ([Directive 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/790/oj)) [P]. It bites on Goodfetch Cloud returning article text whatever the reset (#154 note, question 11); the reset adds that the text came from behind the publisher's paid offer. [I]

## ADR 0052: what changes the rating

| Feature | Effect | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Person-approved per domain, with a warning, recorded in the Audit log | Lowers Goodfetch's exposure; raises the user's | The person chooses the targets. *Anne Frank Fonds* treats a VPN as a lawful tool whose provider does not itself communicate the work (¶62); *Reddit* treats the customer who sets "when, how, and at what scale" as the circumventor (at 48); the Ninth Circuit's *Amazon* opinion calls the agent a tool used by its user. The warning makes the user's act knowing (Art. 6(1), "sciemment") and rules out §1203(c)(5)'s innocent-violator reduction. |
| Generic: no vendor named | Lowers the marketing prong only | §1201(a)(2)(C), Art. 6(2)(a), § 95a(3) Nr. 1 and L.335-3-1 II 4° turn on marketing; the design prong turns on function. |
| Not presented as a meter reset | Weak | The trigger, a paywall that withholds content, is stated in the public ADR. Wording keeps the marketing prong off, not the design prong. |
| At most once per page | Small | Prevents loops and anything that looks like brute force. It does not bound scale: one act per withheld page. |
| Never on a logged-in Session | Lowers | Keeps the reset away from password gates (*Bluetouff*'s login page, *Power Ventures*, *Amazon*), from clickwrap terms a subscriber accepted, and from the measure *Anne Frank Fonds* ¶53 treats as clearly effective. |
| Never on an `identified` Session | Neutral | Right for ADR 0045's reasons, but it means every reset happens in a Session that presents as an ordinary browser (`standard`) or a masked one (`stealth`). It is not a legal safeguard. |
| Never a block, no escalation | Lowers | Keeps the reset out of ADR 0021's rotate-on-block path, the *Reddit* pattern. |
| **Gap:** the new Session's proxy and Identity profile are not fixed | Raises | Each Session binds one proxy, and rotation is on whenever more than one proxy is configured (ADR 0021); in `stealth`, a new Session draws a generated Identity profile (ADR 0035). The fresh Session can therefore change IP address and fingerprint as well as cookies. That is identity disguise, the conduct *Reddit* called "avoid" and "bypass". |
| **Gap:** no stop on objection | Raises for U and B at scale | *3Taps*, *Power Ventures* and *Bluetouff*'s *maintien* turn on continuing after the site said no. |
| **Gap:** the reset cannot be locked on its own | Raises for B | Goodfetch Cloud can lock `wall_domains` empty, but it cannot offer reading what was served while locking only the reset, the most exposed of ADR 0052's three means. |

**Why the reset is the most exposed of the three means [I].** Reading what the server sent (the #154 note's L1) uses content already delivered: *Lexmark*'s open door, and *Emmerich*'s holding. Letting the Filter lists block a Wall's script relies on third-party lists that already do it in every uBlock Origin. The reset is the only means that makes the server send what it was withholding, through an affirmative act triggered by the paywall.

### Changes that would lower the rating

Input for ADR 0052, not a decision. Ranked by effect.

1. **Reset the site's stored state, never the identity.** In "One fresh Session per page", say: "When a paywall leaves the served page without its main content, the engine fetches the page once more in a new Session that keeps the retired one's Access mode, Identity profile and proxy exit, with an empty cookie jar and storage. When the proxy exit cannot be kept, as with a rotating pool without sticky sessions, the page stays as served, with the reason recorded." ADR 0035's saved identities already regenerate an Identity profile from its seed and keep a sticky exit, so the mechanism exists. Effect: the reset stays a cookie reset, which the #154 note rated Low, and never becomes the IP shifting and fresh fingerprints *Reddit* called circumvention. A meter that counts by IP address or fingerprint keeps counting, and the engine accepts that. Removes footnote 4's H / M / M.
2. **Make the reset separately lockable, and lock it in Goodfetch Cloud.** In "Who allows it", add: "Operator policy can allow `wall_domains` while locking the fresh-Session step off." Goodfetch Cloud then offers the rest of the allowance without the reset, unless a publisher's authorization covers the domain. Effect: B's cell for the reset becomes L where it is locked; what remains in the Cloud is the exposure the #154 note gave reading served content and blocking scripts.
3. **Stop on objection.** Add: "A block on the new Session's first request (a 401, 403 or 429, a challenge or a login page) ends the reset for that domain for the rest of the run and is not escalated. An objection recorded in Operator policy for a domain removes it from `wall_domains`; only the operator role can add it back." Effect: keeps the reset out of the *3Taps*, *Power Ventures* and *Lowson* pattern and out of *Bluetouff*'s *maintien*, and gives the Cloud the revocation handling the #65 note recommended.
4. **Keep the docs factual and the tests on the Proving ground.** Describe the step as "fetches the page again without the site's stored cookies and storage". Never write "meter", "free articles", "reset the counter", a publisher's name or a count of articles gained. Keep the metered-paywall conformance test on the Proving ground (ADR 0025), as the ADR already does. Effect: keeps the marketing prong off; it does not change the design prong.
5. **Correct ADR 0052's premise.** Replace "Clearing one's own cookies rates Low too, as long as it is not presented as a meter reset" with a sentence saying that the reset rates Medium because it fires on a paywall, and that clearing cookies rates Low only as a generic control.

**What none of these changes.** The reset fires only when a paywall withholds content, so its function is to get past a meter, and A stays at M / M / M whatever the wording. Only removing the trigger brings A back to L, by leaving a fresh Session to the user's own Hook, the generic means the #154 note rated Low. One variant was considered and is not recommended: not keeping a domain's cookies from one page to the next, which Scrapy's docs advise so "a session identifier does not tie all your requests together" ([Scrapy, Common Practices](https://docs.scrapy.org/en/latest/topics/practices.html)) [P]. It has no paywall trigger and no reactive act, but it also drops challenge-clearance cookies, so more pages would meet a challenge and need the Challenge solver, which the #65 note rated higher. As specified, no rating for the public framework or a user reaches High, so under #192's rule the reset is not reopened; the High is in Goodfetch Cloud, which ADR 0052 already leaves to the cloud map.

## Questions for a lawyer

1. Is a cookie or localStorage meter a measure that "effectively controls access" under §1201(a)(3)(B), when a visitor without state is let in and RFC 6265 lets the browser drop state at any time? Does *Reddit*'s "bulk access" reasoning (at 39–40), or *CoxCom*'s billing-counter holding, make the answer different for one reader and for a run that resets on every page?
2. Is a server-side count keyed to a cookie identifier treated differently from a client-side count, for §1201, InfoSoc Art. 6(3) and CPI L.331-5? After *Anne Frank Fonds* ¶54, is any meter that depends on the browser returning an identifier "effective" in the EU?
3. Is a metered paywall "contrôlée par les titulaires de droits grâce à l'application d'un code d'accès [ou] d'un procédé de protection" (CPI L.331-5)? If so, which offense fits a person whose engine refetches a page without the counted state: L.335-3-1 I or R.335-3?
4. Does a component that refetches a page in a fresh Session only when a paywall withholds the content count as "primarily designed" (§1201(a)(2)(A)), "primarily designed, produced, adapted" (InfoSoc Art. 6(2)(c)), "angepasst" (§ 95a(3) Nr. 3 UrhG) or "spécialement adapté" (L.335-3-1 II), although it names no vendor and the docs never call it a meter reset? Would a GitHub §1201 notice reach the whole repository or that component?
5. Does a person's per-domain approval with a warning shift the act to that person for Goodfetch's purposes (*Anne Frank Fonds* ¶62, *Amazon v. Perplexity*), and how does it affect the person's own position under Art. 6(1) "knowingly", L.335-3-1 "sciemment" and §1203(c)(5)?
6. If the fresh Session also changes IP address or fingerprint, is the reset identity disguise in *Reddit*'s sense? Is keeping the same proxy exit and Identity profile enough to keep it a cookie reset?
7. After a cease-and-desist, or a block that follows a reset, does continuing to reset on that domain create CFAA or §502 liability (*3Taps*, *Power Ventures*, *Lowson*), or *maintien frauduleux* under Code pénal 323-1? What notice handling should Goodfetch Cloud adopt?
8. Do terms like the New York Times' §4.1(4) or Dow Jones' 9.4 bind an automated client that never clicked anything (*Register.com*, *Nguyen*, *Meta v. Bright Data*; Code civil 1119), and does §301 preempt a claim based on them (*X Corp. v. Bright Data*)?
