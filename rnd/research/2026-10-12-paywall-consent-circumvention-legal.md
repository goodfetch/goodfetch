# Legal exposure of paywall and consent-wall circumvention

> **Not legal advice.** This is desk research by a non-lawyer, written to prepare a conversation with counsel. It maps where exposure appears to concentrate and lists questions to ask. It does not say any option is lawful or unlawful. No court has yet ruled on a §1201, CFAA, InfoSoc or CPI claim about a news paywall; most of the record is takedown notices, pleadings and inference. A US, EU or French lawyer may read it differently.

**Research of October 12, 2026.** Ticket [#154](https://github.com/Jbmanllr/automan/issues/154), child of map [#61](https://github.com/Jbmanllr/automan/issues/61); feeds [#155](https://github.com/Jbmanllr/automan/issues/155). Builds on [#65](https://github.com/Jbmanllr/automan/issues/65) and its note ([2026-10-10-stealth-legal-exposure.md](https://github.com/Jbmanllr/automan/blob/research/stealth-legal-exposure/rnd/research/2026-10-10-stealth-legal-exposure.md)), whose stealth and CAPTCHA findings (*Reddit v. SerpApi*, *Google v. SerpApi*, *Corley*, *Van Buren*, *hiQ*, *3Taps*, CPI L.335-3-1, L.331-5, R.335-3, Code pénal 323-3-1, *Anne Frank Fonds* C-788/24, CNIL and EDPB on scraping) are not repeated here.

**Source tags:** **[P]** primary source (statute, opinion, docket, regulator text, takedown notice, first-party docs or code). **[P\*]** standard primary link given from knowledge, not re-fetched this session. **[C]** commentary or press, used where the primary text was not read. **[V]** vendor or trade-body self-description. **[I]** inference. **[U]** unverified.

**Context in Goodfetch today.** On `main`, Goodfetch already does none of levels 2 to 5 and refuses level 1 for paywalls: no cosmetic rule hides a paywall vendor's overlay or a section marked `isAccessibleForFree: false`, and paywall vendors' scripts are on the never-block list (ADR 0047). A 402 or paywall redirect is `payment_required` and is never escalated, rotated or retried past (ADR 0045). Consent prompts are refused; a consent wall is `consent_required` and is accepted only on a person-approved `consent_domains` (ADR 0048). ADR 0018 lets a capability leave the public framework, as a Private component, only when publishing it would itself create legal exposure. This note answers what exposure each level would create if [#155](https://github.com/Jbmanllr/automan/issues/155) made it opt-in.

## Summary

- **Exposure follows naming and marketing more than capability** [I]. Every enforcement action found targeted a tool *named and documented* as a paywall bypass: Bypass Paywalls Clean (GitHub §1201 notice by the News/Media Alliance, 3,879 repositories disabled, August 2024; Der Spiegel notices 2024 and 2025; BREIN notice 2026), 12ft.io (host locked at the Alliance's request, July 2025). Generic DOM access, request blocking, header and cookie control have large lawful uses and drew no action. Under §1201(a)(2) and InfoSoc Art. 6(2) the "primarily designed" and "marketed for" prongs are what turn a generic tool into a trafficked device. [P]
- **The US has no paywall ruling.** The closest is *Ziff Davis v. OpenAI* (S.D.N.Y., Dec. 15, 2025): robots.txt is not an access control, and circumvention needs an affirmative act that "disables or voids" a measure; the court noted Ziff Davis does not typically use paywalls. Removing an overlay or refusing to run a gate script is an affirmative "remove/deactivate" act, so it is closer to circumvention than ignoring robots.txt. The best defense for content already in the HTML is *MDY v. Blizzard* / *Lexmark*: a measure that leaves "another route wide open" does not effectively control access. [P]
- **France names press publications in its TPM statute.** CPI L.331-5 protects effective technical measures over works "et … d'une publication de presse", so a paywall is squarely the kind of measure L.335-3-1 protects, subject to "effectiveness". Germany has treated a gate script (Bild's anti-adblock wall) as an effective TPM under § 95a UrhG (LG Hamburg, *BILDsmart*; citation unverified). [P]/[C]
- **Automated consent is not GDPR consent, but the bigger exposure is contract.** A bot's click is no data subject's "indication of wishes" (GDPR Art. 4(11), 7). When nobody is behind the bot the privacy harm is mostly the site's compliance problem; when Goodfetch acts for a real person (agent use) it manufactures invalid consent. Clicking "accept" on a consent-or-pay wall likely binds the operator to the site's terms (*Register.com v. Verio*; e-Commerce Directive Arts. 9 to 11; Code civil 1127-1), which also forfeits the "not a user" defense of *Meta v. Bright Data*. [P]+[I]
- **Identity disguise (level 4) is the most exposed level after a bundled solver.** *Reddit v. SerpApi* already held masking (proxies, UA spoofing) to be circumvention on the pleadings; posing as Googlebot to get content served only to verified crawlers adds deception (Google says a UA string proves nothing and documents reverse-DNS verification). In France it resembles *Bluetouff* (Code pénal 323-1). Clearing one's own cookies to reset a meter is weakly exposed in isolation but systematic at scale in a cloud. [P]+[I]
- **Fetching elsewhere (level 5) moves the risk from circumvention to copyright.** *Healthcare Advocates v. Harding* held that requesting archived copies is not bypassing a measure, but the archived copy of a paywalled article is likely unauthorized; in the EU a profit-making operator is presumed to know (*GS Media*), and *ACI Adam* and DSM Art. 4's "lawfully accessible" condition keep the TDM exception away from it. The Alliance's 2024 notice named automatic archive lookup as one of BPC's circumvention methods. archive.today is under an FBI subpoena (Oct. 2025) and was blacklisted by English Wikipedia (Feb. 2026). [P]/[C]
- **Goodfetch Cloud is the direct actor at every level**, and the only placement where controls (allowlists, publisher-authorization checks, logs, kill switch) work. A Private component does not change the "provides/traffics" or "offrir/mettre à disposition" characterization; it lowers visibility only. A user's own Plugin on a neutral interface puts the act on the user, unless Goodfetch ships presets or docs that market bypass. [I]
- **The market splits the same way.** Scraping platforms ship levels 1 and 3 (often accepting), generic header/cookie/proxy controls for level 4, and nothing named for levels 2, 4 or 5. Only Bright Data's own blog markets "bypassing paywalls", one Apify Store actor offers "Googlebot headers", and the named bypass tools (BPC, 12ft, 13ft, smry's past, removepaywall) are the ones taken down. uBlock Origin's uAssets and AdGuard explicitly refuse to bypass paywalls. [P]

## Levels and placements

| Level | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **L1** | Reads what the server already sent: removes a soft paywall's or consent wall's overlay and extracts the content in the HTML. |
| **L2** | Blocks the gate's own script (Piano/tinypass, Sourcepoint, Didomi, Poool, Zephr) so the wall never renders. |
| **L3** | Accepts consent walls automatically, outside a person-approved `consent_domains`. |
| **L4** | Resets or disguises identity: clears cookies or opens a new Session to reset a meter, poses as Googlebot, spoofs the referrer. |
| **L5** | Fetches the content elsewhere: archive services, cached copies. |

| Code | Placement | Who acts on the site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Public framework, `MIT OR Apache-2.0` (GitHub, crates.io, npm, PyPI) | The user; Goodfetch publishes |
| **C1** | Private component, closed module licensed to vetted self-hosting customers (ADR 0018) | The licensee; Goodfetch provides |
| **P** | A user's own Plugin; the public framework only exposes the generic hooks it uses | The user, who also authors it |
| **B** | Goodfetch Cloud operates it (a French company assumed) | Goodfetch Cloud, for a customer |

## Risk map [I]

Ratings are US / EU / FR. L = Low, M = Medium, H = High. "Named" means the feature, preset, flag or docs name paywalls, paywall vendors, Googlebot or archives as a way past a wall; "generic" means only general hooks (DOM edit, request blocking, header and cookie control) with no bypass presets or marketing.

| Level | A public framework | C1 Private component | P user's Plugin | B Goodfetch Cloud |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **L1 overlay removal** | generic **L / L / L**; named **M / L / M** | **M / L / M** | **L / L / L** | **M / M / M** |
| **L2 gate-script blocking** | generic **L / L / L**; named **M / M / M** | **M / H / H** | **L / L / L** | **H / H / H** |
| **L3 auto-accept consent walls** | **L / L / L** | **L / L / L** | **L / L / M**¹ | **M / M / M** |
| **L4 identity reset or disguise** | cookie reset **L / L / L**; Googlebot, referrer or meter-reset named **H / M / M** | **H / H / H** | **M / L / M**² | **H / H / H** |
| **L5 archives and caches** | generic **L / L / L**; named archive lookup **M / M / M** | **M / M / M** | **L / L / L** | **M / H / H** |

¹ Medium in France when the Plugin acts for a real person: the "consent" it gives is invalid and the site relies on it. ² Medium where Goodfetch's docs or examples provide spoofing presets the Plugin uses.

**Why the placements differ.**
- **A and C1** are trafficking cases. §1201(a)(2) reaches anyone who "offer[s] to the public, provide[s], or otherwise traffic[s]" in a device primarily designed or marketed for circumvention ([17 U.S.C. §1201](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1201)) [P]; InfoSoc Art. 6(2) and CPI L.335-3-1 II reach devices "promoted, advertised or marketed" for it or "primarily designed" for it ([Directive 2001/29/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2001/29/oj)) [P\*]. Licensing a closed module to customers is still providing it, and paid supply weighs against the provider; in the EU and France a closed module whose only purpose is circumvention also loses the "other commercial uses" argument that *Nintendo v. PC Box* keeps open for general tools ([C-355/12](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-355/12)) [P\*]. That is why C1 rates at or above a named public feature, not below. [I]
- **P** puts the act on the Plugin's author and user. Goodfetch exposes only hooks with large non-circumvention uses (ad blocking, header control, cookie management), and §1201(c)(3) says no product has to be designed to respond to any particular measure ([§1201](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1201)) [P]. The Plugin's user is a direct circumventor (US §1201(a)(1); FR petty offense R.335-3, prior note). [P]
- **B** makes Goodfetch the direct actor under §1201(a)(1), a "service" trafficker under (a)(2), the party to any terms it accepts, the reproducer of the content, and, in France, the person who "neutralise" the measure (L.335-3-1 I). It is also where press publishers' right (DSM Art. 15) bites, since Goodfetch Cloud is an information-society service provider. [P\*]+[I]

**What the map suggests for [#155](https://github.com/Jbmanllr/automan/issues/155) (input, not a decision) [I].**
- Generic hooks in the public framework are low risk at every level; named bypass features are not. ADR 0018's rule therefore bites on *named* features: a named L2 vendor list, L4 Googlebot or meter-reset preset, or L5 archive fallback would leave the public framework if it existed at all.
- Moving a named feature to C1 does not lower exposure; in the EU and France it raises it for L2 and L4. A Private component is not a safe harbor for paywall circumvention the way it partly is for gated stealth.
- L3 is the one level where the risk is contract and GDPR, not circumvention, and the existing `consent_domains` control (a person approves the domain) is the recognized mitigation: it makes the acceptance a person's act.
- For B, every level beyond reading a publicly served page makes Goodfetch Cloud the actor; the only defensible cloud posture found in the market is publisher authorization (licensed access, Pay per crawl, a customer's own subscription login), which ADR 0045's paid-fetch path already supports.

## United States

### Statute

- §1201(a)(1)(A) bans circumventing a measure that "effectively controls access" to a work; (a)(2) bans trafficking in devices or services primarily designed, of limited other commercial use, or marketed for it; (a)(3)(A) defines circumvent as to "avoid, bypass, remove, deactivate, or impair"; (a)(3)(B) makes a measure effective if "in the ordinary course of its operation" it requires the application of information, a process or a treatment. §1201(c)(3): no device need be designed to respond to any particular measure. [P] https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1201
- §1203(c)(3)(A): statutory damages of $200 to $2,500 "per act of circumvention, device, product, component, offer, or service"; tripled for repeat violations within three years, reducible for innocent violators. [P] https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1203
- Copyright statutory damages up to $150,000 per work for willful infringement. [P\*] https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/504
- The Copyright Office's TDM exemption (37 CFR 201.40(b)(5), last amended 89 FR 85446, Oct. 28, 2024) covers only researchers at nonprofit higher-education institutions, lawfully acquired works and secured corpora. It does not reach commercial scraping or a cloud service. [P] https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/201.40

### Is a paywall an access control?

- **No ruling on a news paywall was found.** [I] Tracker: https://nortonlaw.com/dmca-tracker/ [C]
- *Ziff Davis v. OpenAI*, S.D.N.Y. 1:25-cv-04315-SHS, opinion of Dec. 15, 2025: §1201(a) claim dismissed. robots.txt does not effectively control access, "any more than a sign requesting that visitors 'keep off the grass'"; circumvention requires affirmatively disabling or voiding a measure. The opinion notes Ziff Davis does not typically place content behind paywalls. [P] https://business.cch.com/ipld/ZiffDavisOpenAi20251215.pdf
- *MDY v. Blizzard* (9th Cir. 2011): Warden did not control access to elements already on the player's disk; quoting *Lexmark*, §1201 "does not naturally extend to a technological measure that restricts one form of access but leaves another route wide open." It did control access to server-side dynamic elements. [P] https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2011/02/17/09-15932.pdf. *Lexmark* (6th Cir. 2004) read only as quoted. [U] https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/788158/lexmark-international-inc-v-static-control-components-inc/
- Mapping [I]: a soft paywall whose article is in the HTML (L1) is the *Lexmark* "open front door"; a paywall whose script fetches the body after an entitlement check (often L2's target) is *MDY*'s server-side element, which was protected.
- Against that: *Universal v. Reimerdes* (S.D.N.Y. 2000, aff'd *Corley*) refused to read "effectively" as "unbeatable"; a measure qualifies if it works in the ordinary course. [P] https://cyber.harvard.edu/openlaw/dvd/ny/trial/op.html. *321 Studios v. MGM* (N.D. Cal. 2004): CSS effective despite leaked keys. [C] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/321_Studios_v._Metro_Goldwyn_Mayer_Studios,_Inc.
- The News/Media Alliance's position, in its GitHub notice, is that its members' soft and hard paywalls are §1201(a)(3)(B) measures. [P] https://github.com/github/dmca/blob/master/2024/08/2024-08-09-news-media-alliance.md

### Publisher suits that mention paywalls

- *NYT v. Microsoft and OpenAI* (S.D.N.Y., filed Dec. 2023): paywall bypass appears as factual allegations (ChatGPT reproducing paywalled text; Browse with Bing), not as a §1201 count as far as read. [P, partly read] https://www.law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/NYT_Complaint_Dec2023.pdf
- *Dow Jones and NY Post v. Perplexity* (S.D.N.Y., Oct. 2024): copying and hallucination claims; paywall as fact. [C] https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/dow-jones-new-york-post-sue-perplexity-ai-over-model-training. Chicago Tribune alleges the Comet browser bypasses its paywall. [C] https://www.contentgrip.com/publishers-sue-perplexity-ai/. No §1201 paywall count confirmed in any Perplexity complaint. [U]
- *Brave v. News Corp* (N.D. Cal. 4:26-cv-04758-JST): News Corp's counterclaims (July 21, 2026) allege "content laundering"; no §1201 paywall count confirmed. [C/P] https://copyrightalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/brave-vs-news-corp-reply-and-counterclaims.pdf
- Canada, for comparison: *Blacklock's v. Canadian Vintners* (2015) found paywall circumvention under Canadian law. [C] https://archive.techdirt.com/articles/20151028/10255032658/canadian-judge-says-asking-copy-legally-obtained-paywalled-article-is-circumvention.shtml

### Contract, CFAA and other theories

- **Bot assent.** *Register.com v. Verio* (2d Cir. 2004): a robot's repeated queries with knowledge of the terms bound the operator. [P] https://www.duanemorris.com/site/static/356_F_3d_393.pdf. *Meta v. Bright Data* (N.D. Cal. Jan. 23, 2024): Meta's terms did not bar logged-off scraping; a logged-off scraper was not a "user" (prior note). An automated click on "I agree" (L3) is an affirmative act, stronger than browsewrap, so it likely imports the site's anti-scraping and no-reproduction clauses and gives up the "not a user" defense. [I]
- **CFAA and Cal. Penal Code 502.** Content served openly is weak ground after *Van Buren* (prior note). After a cease-and-desist plus IP blocking, continued access is "without permission" (*Facebook v. Power Ventures*, 9th Cir. 2016). [C] https://cases.justia.com/federal/district-courts/california/candce/5:2008cv05780/210110/90/0.pdf. Clearing one's own cookies has no case law. [C] https://ashkansoltani.org/2013/07/25/how-protecting-your-privacy-could-make-you-the-bad-guy/
- **Googlebot impersonation.** Google says to verify Googlebot by reverse then forward DNS, or its published IP ranges, and warns of requests "claiming to be from Google". [P] https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/verifying-googlebot. Paywalled pages should mark paid sections with `isAccessibleForFree: false`, which "helps Google differentiate paywalled content from the practice of cloaking". [P] https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/paywalled-content. Flexible sampling (metering, lead-in) is Google's sanctioned model. [P] https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/flexible-sampling. No Lanham Act §43(a) case on Googlebot spoofing was found; the claim looks weak for lack of consumer confusion. [P] https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1125 + [I]. The stronger theories are §1201 "avoid/bypass" by masking (*Reddit v. SerpApi*, prior note) and fraud. [I]
- **US privacy.** No general cookie-consent duty; CCPA regulations make businesses honor opt-out preference signals such as GPC ([11 CCR §7025](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/11-CCR-7025)). Auto-accepting creates no US privacy liability for the operator; sending GPC is lawful. [P]+[I]
- **Hot news.** *Barclays v. Theflyonthewall* (2d Cir. 2011) keeps the claim narrow; rarely viable against a framework. [C] https://www.dmlp.org/node/5772
- **Archives.** *Healthcare Advocates v. Harding* (E.D. Pa. 2007): requesting copies through the Wayback Machine "is not avoiding or bypassing the measure"; "lack of permission is not circumvention". [P] https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-paed-2_05-cv-03524/pdf/USCOURTS-paed-2_05-cv-03524-0.pdf

## European Union and France

- **France: press publications are named.** CPI L.331-5 protects effective technical measures restricting unauthorized uses of works "et … d'une publication de presse"; Arcom has a regulatory and monitoring mission over technical measures (L.331-12 3°, L.331-28). [P] https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/section_lc/LEGITEXT000006069414/LEGISCTA000006161657/. Penalties for providing (L.335-3-1 II) and using (R.335-3) circumvention are in the prior note.
- **Germany: a gate script held to be a TPM.** LG Hamburg (*Bild v. Richter*, "BILDsmart") held Bild's anti-adblock wall an effective measure under § 95a UrhG; distributing code to get around it infringed § 95a(3). Case number, date and appeal not verified. [C] https://www.lto.de/recht/hintergruende/h/bild-de-adblocker-sperre-umgehung-adblock-eyeo; https://www.dr-datenschutz.de/bildsmart-fragwuerdiges-urteil-des-lg-hamburg-zur-adblock-sperre/
- **Germany: ad blocking.** BGH *Werbeblocker II* (I ZR 154/16, Apr. 19, 2018): offering Adblock Plus is neither targeted obstruction nor aggressive practice under the UWG. [C] https://rewis.io/urteile/urteil/gzl-19-04-2018-i-zr-15416/. BGH *Werbeblocker IV* (I ZR 131/23, July 31, 2025) remanded the copyright question whether a blocker that acts on the DOM/CSSOM alters a protected program (§ 69c UrhG); no OLG ruling found. [C] https://www.lto.de/recht/nachrichten/n/bgh-izr13123-werbeblocker-urheberrechte-umarbeitungsrechte. Counterweight: CJEU *Sony v. Datel* (C-159/23, Oct. 17, 2024), variable data in RAM is not a protected program. [P] https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2024-10/cp240181en.pdf
- **Germany: scraping after a checkbox.** BGH *Flugvermittlung* (I ZR 224/12, Apr. 30, 2014): screen scraping is not unfair under the UWG even after a terms checkbox, which is not a technical protection; overcoming a real technical protection could make it unfair. [C] https://www.otto-schmidt.de/news/wirtschaftsrecht/zur-frage-der-zulassigkeit-des-sogenannten-screen-scraping-2014-04-30.html
- **Republishing metered content.** OLG München 29 U 953/16 (July 14, 2016): copying metered-paywall content onto another site can be making available (§ 19a UrhG). [C] https://www.haufe.de/id/entscheidungen/olg-muenchen-urteil-vom-14072016-29-u-95316-HI9728478.html
- **France: no paywall-circumvention ruling found**, and no French or German court ruling against a paywall-bypass extension. [U, searches negative]
- **Consent (L3).** GDPR Art. 4(11) requires a data subject's "freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous indication" by "clear affirmative action"; Art. 7 puts the burden of proof on the controller. [P\*] https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj. *Planet49* (C-673/17) requires active consent [P\*] https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-673/17; *Orange Romania* (C-61/19) on proof [P\*] https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-61/19; EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 [P\*] https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-052020-consent-under-regulation-2016679_en. No authority on a bot's consent was found; a click no person made is no one's indication of wishes. [I]. ePrivacy Art. 5(3) protects the "subscriber or user", a natural person ([2002/58/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2002/58/oj)) [P\*], so when no person is behind the session the harm falls mostly on the site's records. [I]. Germany's TDDDG § 26 and its consent-management ordinance (in force Apr. 1, 2025) admit delegated consent only through recognized services. [C] https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/cookies-einwilligungsverwaltung-verordnung.html. GDPR Art. 21(5) allows objection by automated means, which supports refusing by default. [P\*]
- **Cookie walls in France.** Conseil d'État n°434684 (June 19, 2020) annulled CNIL's blanket ban, leaving case-by-case review. [P] https://www.cnil.fr/cookies-et-autres-traceurs-le-conseil-detat-rend-sa-decision-sur-les-lignes-directrices-de-la-cnil. CNIL's evaluation criteria (May 16, 2022) center on a fair alternative, such as paid access. [P] https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-et-autres-traceurs/regles/cookie-walls/la-cnil-publie-des-premiers-criteres-devaluation. EDPB Opinion 08/2024 on consent-or-pay for large platforms. [P] https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-04/edpb_opinion_202408_consentorpay_en.pdf
- **Contract formed by automated acceptance.** e-Commerce Directive Arts. 9 to 11 ([2000/31/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2000/31/oj)) and Code civil 1127-1 ff. and 1103 ([Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/texte_lc/LEGITEXT000006070721)) validate electronic contracting [P\*]; *Ryanair* (C-30/14) lets terms bind where the Database Directive does not apply (prior note).
- **Identity disguise (L4).** *Bluetouff* (Cass. crim., May 20, 2015, n°14-81.336) punished staying in a system after learning it was protected. [C] https://next.ink/18233/95165-affaire-bluetouff-cour-cassation-consacre-vol-fichiers-informatiques/. Faking a crawler identity to obtain content reserved to verified crawlers shows stronger intent. [I]. Database right: Directive 96/9/EC Art. 7, CPI L.342-1; *CV-Online* (C-762/19) requires substantial extraction and risk to investment; *Innoweb* (C-202/12) on re-utilization by meta-search. [P\*] https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-762/19; https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-202/12. Systematic meter resets by a hosted service fit the investment-harm pattern. [I]
- **Copies held elsewhere (L5).** *Svensson* (C-466/12, ¶31): a link that circumvents restrictions reaches a new public. *GS Media* (C-160/15): knowledge of illegality presumed for profit-making actors. *Renckhoff* (C-161/17): reposting a copy is a new communication. *ACI Adam* (C-435/12): private copying does not cover unlawful sources. [P\*] https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-160/15; https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-161/17; https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-435/12. DSM Directive Art. 4 TDM needs "lawful access" ([2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/790/oj), Recital 14) [P\*]; an archive copy of a paywalled article is unlikely to qualify. [I]. OLG Hamburg *LAION* (5 U 104/24, Dec. 10, 2025) confirmed the § 44b TDM exception and held a natural-language reservation in terms of use not machine-readable; further appeal allowed. [P] https://justiz.hamburg.de/resource/blob/1126574/2495057737fed4883eec42dce4d2430b/ki-und-urheberrecht-hanseatisches-oberlandesgericht-weist-berufung-zurueck-data.pdf

## Questions for a lawyer

1. Is a soft paywall whose article is in the HTML an access control that "effectively controls access" under §1201(a)(3)(B), given *MDY*/*Lexmark* against *Reimerdes*? Does the answer change when the body is fetched by the gate script after an entitlement check?
2. Does a public, generic request-blocking or DOM-editing hook stay outside §1201(a)(2) and InfoSoc Art. 6(2) if the project never names paywall vendors, and what wording in docs or examples would cross into "marketed for"?
3. Would a GitHub §1201 notice like the Alliance's reach the whole Goodfetch repository (and forks) if one named feature existed, and how should the repository be structured to limit that?
4. Does supplying a named feature as a Private component to vetted licensees change the analysis under §1201(a)(2), L.335-3-1 II and § 95a(3) UrhG, or only the evidence?
5. Under CPI L.331-5, is a press publisher's soft paywall an "effective" measure whose use is "contrôlée par les titulaires de droits"? Is there any French decision on press paywalls?
6. When Goodfetch Cloud clicks "accept" on a consent-or-pay wall, is it bound by the site's terms (France, EU, US), and who is controller for the resulting processing?
7. When a Plugin or the Cloud acts for a real person, is an automated acceptance an invalid consent that exposes Goodfetch, and does `consent_domains` (a person approves per domain) make it the person's own act?
8. Is resetting a cookie meter in a fresh Session circumvention in the US, EU or France, or only a contract matter? Does doing it systematically at scale change that?
9. Does posing as Googlebot or spoofing a referrer expose the operator under Code pénal 323-1, § 5 UWG, fraud, or §1201's "avoid/bypass" after *Reddit v. SerpApi*?
10. Is fetching an archive.today or Wayback copy of a paywalled article a reproduction or communication of an unlawful copy for a commercial operator (*GS Media*), and does it bar DSM Art. 4 TDM?
11. Does DSM Art. 15 (press publishers' right) apply to Goodfetch Cloud returning article text to a customer?
12. Is the publisher-authorization model (licensed access, Pay per crawl, the customer's own subscription session) a complete answer for the Cloud at every level?

- *BILDsmart* case number, date and appeal; OLG Hamburg's ruling after *Werbeblocker IV*; any French paywall ruling; any EU ruling on bot consent or Googlebot spoofing: not found or not verified.
