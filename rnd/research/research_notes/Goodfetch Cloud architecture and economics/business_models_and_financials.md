# Business models, monetization structures and financial benchmarks for Goodfetch Cloud (as of 2026-10-12)

_Research notes, desk research only. Labels on figures: **[D]** disclosed by the company or in a filing; **[P]** press report quoting the company or sources; **[E]** third-party estimate (Sacra, GetLatka and similar); **[V]** vendor marketing claim; **[C]** my own calculation from cited figures. About 50 web searches ran before the session's shared search budget was exhausted; the rest of the evidence comes from direct fetches of primary pages (pricing pages, docs, filings, press releases). "Not found" therefore means "not found in these searches and fetches", not "does not exist". Jargon used below: ARR = annual recurring revenue (a run rate, not audited revenue); GM = gross margin (revenue minus cost of revenue, as % of revenue); MoR = merchant of record (the reseller that is legally the seller and handles VAT, fraud and disputes); PPE = pay per event (Apify's per-event billing for Store Actors); BYOC = bring your own cloud (vendor software operated in the customer's cloud account); ZDR = zero data retention._

## 1. Business model of each company: what they sell, how they package it, and who sells open-core features, self-hosted licenses, BYOC or dedicated deployments, marketplace listings, datasets or managed services

### Takeaway
Nearly every company in the set gives away an engine or SDK and sells **operated capacity metered by usage** (browser time, pages or credits, requests, proxy bytes, model tokens) through a ladder of self-serve plans topped by a sales-led enterprise tier, and that tier sells **security and compliance**: SSO, ZDR, SLAs, HIPAA BAAs, private cloud or VPC. Paid self-hosting licenses survive only where the engine is not permissive (Browserless under SSPL, Lightpanda under AGPL) or as an enterprise "private deployment" line. The largest revenue pools sit with access-network companies (Bright Data, Oxylabs, Zyte) that stack APIs, datasets and managed data services on top of proxy networks. Apify is the only scraping company with a scale marketplace.

### Cited Findings

**Actor platform and marketplace (Apify)**
- Apify plans (Free, Starter, Scale, Business, Enterprise) turn the monthly fee into **prepaid platform usage**. Unused prepaid usage "expire[s] at the end of the billing cycle". Overage on paid plans is billed on the next invoice up to a usage limit the customer configures, and free accounts are blocked rather than billed. One compute unit (CU) equals 1 GB of RAM for one hour, and the CU price falls with each higher plan. Residential proxy is billed per GB at a price that also falls by tier. Concurrency and RAM add-ons exist, and paid plans get tiered Store discounts (Bronze, Silver, Gold) — [Apify pricing](https://apify.com/pricing), fetched 2026-10-12.
- Apify now lets **autonomous agents pay without an account** via x402 or MPP at agi.apify.com, with a temporary API token that carries a fixed spend cap — [Apify pricing](https://apify.com/pricing).
- Store monetization for developers: pay per event (PPE), where developer profit = 0.8 × revenue − platform costs, or pay per usage, where the developer adds no fee — [Apify docs, pricing and costs](https://docs.apify.com/actors/publishing/monetize/pricing-and-costs.md). Revenue and costs from free-plan users are excluded: Apify covers their platform usage — [same](https://docs.apify.com/actors/publishing/monetize/pricing-and-costs.md).
- The flat monthly **rental** model is being retired. Since April 1, developers can no longer publish new rental Actors or change rental pricing, and rental Actors are "fully retired" on October 1 — [Apify docs, rental](https://docs.apify.com/actors/publishing/monetize/rental.md). The year is 2026 per [AgentByline](https://agentbyline.com/articles/apify-actor-passive-income-what-really-earns-in-2026-67lcfr).
- Per-request agentic payments (for example Skyfire) require PPE, charging only for events (not platform usage), limited permissions, no Standby mode, and **developer identity verification (KYC)** — [Apify docs, monetize](https://docs.apify.com/platform/actors/publishing/monetize).
- "Open Source Fair Share" pays open-source maintainers who wrap their project as an Actor a 20–30% affiliate commission on new customers, capped at $2,500 per customer (the FAQ says 20% recurring). Apify "handles hosting, billing, payments, and taxes" — [Apify open source fair share](https://apify.com/partners/open-source-fair-share).
- In Apify's own survey, 97.1% of the 64,812 Store Actors used pay-per-event (local corpus item `reviews-reports-59`) — [pricing-signals.md](/Users/jb/Dev/automan/rnd/research/market/pricing-signals.md).

**Crawl and extraction APIs**
- **Firecrawl**: the engine is AGPL-3.0 — [GitHub](https://github.com/mendableai/firecrawl) — and Firecrawl moved its SDKs to MIT so that closed products could embed them — [ADR 0018](/Users/jb/Dev/automan/docs/adr/0018-permissive-license-private-components-only-for-legal-exposure.md). Credits are shared across endpoints: 1 credit equals one basic scrape, crawl or map page; JSON, Question and Highlight formats add 4 credits per page; Interact (browser) costs 2 credits per browser minute; the Agent endpoint uses dynamic pricing after 5 free daily runs. Credits do not roll over, except one month on Scale. Pay-as-you-go top-ups come in $5 increments up to a monthly spend limit the user sets, and they "expire if you cancel your subscription". A scrape with no result is not charged, but a 403 or 404 page costs 1 credit. Enterprise adds ZDR, SSO and SCIM, static egress IPs, an SLA and invoiced billing. The pricing page gives no self-hosting price — [Firecrawl pricing](https://www.firecrawl.dev/pricing).
- Firecrawl **Alexandria** (September 2026) is a data library for agents: official data providers, Firecrawl indexes and the live web behind one interface. Firecrawl already pays providers such as Wikimedia Enterprise and plans a self-service system that would pay contributors "based on demand from AI agents" — [PR.com release, 2026-09-22](https://www.pr.com/press-release/979737); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/22/web-scraping-startup-firecrawl-closes-75m-investment/).
- **Spider**: an open-source Rust crate (an "MIT ecosystem" per the [commercial landscape note](/Users/jb/Dev/automan/rnd/research/2026-10-07-commercial-landscape.md), citing [Spider open source](https://spider.cloud/open-source/)) and a cloud sold as pay-as-you-go credits that "never expire". Bandwidth and compute are metered separately. "If Spider returns an error, you are not charged." JS rendering and AI extraction carry no multiplier, proxied bandwidth bills at 1.5x to 4x and ZDR at 2.5x, and model calls are billed by the user's own model provider. Top-ups earn bonus credits, and enterprise gets custom SLAs — [Spider pricing guide](https://spider.cloud/guides/pricing-and-plans/).
- **Crawl4AI**: "The library stays here and stays free." Crawl4AI Cloud is live, with a rate-limited free tier, a paid tier "from $10" and pay-as-you-go — [Crawl4AI docs](https://docs.crawl4ai.com/). The company describes itself as a "Peak XV-backed AI infrastructure startup" based in Singapore [V] — [unclecode.com](https://www.unclecode.com/). Its license adds an attribution clause that reaches public uses — [ADR 0018](/Users/jb/Dev/automan/docs/adr/0018-permissive-license-private-components-only-for-legal-exposure.md).
- **Zyte**: maintains Scrapy (open source) and sells four lines. Zyte API is billed per successful response, priced in five site-difficulty tiers and HTTP versus browser rendering; monthly commitments lower the rate, with "no overage penalties". Zyte Data is a managed service that builds, runs and maintains collection and delivers "schema-ready data to your warehouse". Scrapy Cloud is hosting. There is also an MCP server — [Zyte home](https://www.zyte.com/); [Zyte pricing](https://www.zyte.com/pricing/). After the $5 free credit in the first month, the account is suspended until the user sets a spending limit — [Zyte API pricing docs](https://docs.zyte.com/zyte-api/pricing.html), per search-result extraction.
- **Bright Data**: Web Scraper API (structured data from "800+ sites", pay-per-result), Datasets ("ready-to-use... data from 800+ popular websites"), Scraper Studio (AI-built scrapers with "Self-Healing"), Bright Insights (e-commerce intelligence), SERP API, Web Unlocker and Browser API — [Bright Data products](https://brightdata.com/products). Web Unlocker and SERP API charge only for successful requests, and failed retry attempts are not billed — [Bright Data docs](https://docs.brightdata.com/scraping-automation/web-unlocker/introduction), per search-result extraction. Residential proxies are gated behind KYC (§7).
- **Oxylabs group**: proxy networks and public web data gathering — [The Recursive, 2026-07-10](https://www.therecursive.com/oxylabs-lithuanias-newest-eur3-1b-unicorn-ends-its-bootstrapped-era/). It is developing a web index and a headless browser, and has acquired Webshare (2022) and ScrapingBee (2025) — [Oxylabs blog](https://oxylabs.io/blog/oxylabs-receives-investment). ScrapingBee stays independent, as "a leading direct-to-consumer product" in the group's portfolio — [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/oxylabs-group-expands-market-reach-scrapingbee-acquisition-3776253).

**Hosted browsers for code and agents**
- **Browserbase**: Free, Developer, Startup and Scale plans that meter browser hours, concurrency, a Search API, a Fetch API, proxies per GB and a **Model Gateway at "Market Price: Pay as you go"**. Captcha solving starts at Developer; stealth goes from none to "Basic" to "Advanced" by tier. Scale offers HIPAA (BAA), DPA, SSO and SOC 2, and the FAQ cites private cloud options, VPC connectivity and data residency. Browserbase "doesn't offer on-prem deployment" (it offers hybrid-architecture consulting instead) — [Browserbase pricing](https://www.browserbase.com/pricing). Stagehand is the open-source SDK; Director lets anyone generate Stagehand scripts and deploy them on Browserbase — [Browserbase Series B post, 2025-06-18](https://www.browserbase.com/blog/series-b-and-beyond).
- **Browser Use**: MIT framework — [commercial landscape note](/Users/jb/Dev/automan/rnd/research/2026-10-07-commercial-landscape.md). The cloud is pay-as-you-go: "No subscription. Credits never expire." It bills browser time per minute (1-minute minimum), proxy traffic per GB with a large discount for bring-your-own proxy, and LLM tokens at "**Model cost + 20%**". With bring-your-own-key, it charges 20% of published token rates. Concurrency limits rise with lifetime spend and "never go down". There is a one-time $1 free credit — [Browser Use pricing](https://browser-use.com/pricing).
- **Steel**: steel-browser is Apache-2.0 and self-hostable — [GitHub](https://github.com/steel-dev/steel-browser); [commercial landscape note](/Users/jb/Dev/automan/rnd/research/2026-10-07-commercial-landscape.md). The cloud charges a base fee plus usage: Launch is $0 with a one-time $30 credit grant, Scale is $250 per month with $100 of monthly credits, and Enterprise adds reserved browser pools, 24-hour sessions and uptime SLAs. It meters browser hours, proxy GB, CAPTCHA solves and /scrape calls — [Steel pricing](https://steel.dev/pricing).
- **Kernel**: plans are "plus usage costs". Usage is metered in **GB-seconds of browser memory** "(no charges for idle time or proxies)", and managed-auth connections differentiate the tiers. Enterprise lists a 99.9% SLA, SOC 2 and HIPAA, white-labeling and ZDR. The open-source part is the `kernel-images` repository — [Kernel pricing](https://www.kernel.sh/pricing).
- **Browserless**: dual-licensed "SSPL-1.0 OR Browserless Commercial License". Building commercial sites or apps, or running it in closed-source CI, requires the commercial license — [Browserless LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/browserless/browserless/main/LICENSE). The cloud meters units of up to 30 seconds of browser time per connection (a reconnect starts a new unit), proxy units per MB and 10 units per successful captcha solve. Enterprise sells "Private deployments or licensed self-hosting", a "Commercial license for self-hosting" and air-gapped licensing — [Browserless pricing](https://www.browserless.io/pricing).
- **Lightpanda**: the default license is "AGPL-3.0-only" — [LICENSING.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lightpanda-io/browser/main/LICENSING.md). The cloud is metered in browser hours: Explorer is free with a monthly allowance, Builder is $19 per month plus overage, and Enterprise offers "On premise or private cloud options" with SLAs — [Lightpanda pricing](https://lightpanda.io/pricing). The prior license research found that Lightpanda also sells a commercial license and keeps multi-context tabs and sandboxing for paying customers — [ADR 0018](/Users/jb/Dev/automan/docs/adr/0018-permissive-license-private-components-only-for-legal-exposure.md).
- **Skyvern**: AGPL-3.0 — [GitHub](https://github.com/Skyvern-AI/skyvern) — with cloud credit plans (Free, Hobby, Pro, Enterprise). Enterprise lists HIPAA, a SOC-2 report and human-in-the-loop. The page shows no outcome-based pricing — [Skyvern pricing](https://www.skyvern.com/pricing).
- **Hyperbrowser** hosts Browser Use — [ADR 0018](/Users/jb/Dev/automan/docs/adr/0018-permissive-license-private-components-only-for-legal-exposure.md). Its pricing page rendered no readable content — [Hyperbrowser pricing](https://www.hyperbrowser.ai/pricing).
- **Anchor** sells deployment in the customer's cloud (the closest thing to BYOC in this set), and Kadoa offers on-prem or private cloud — [commercial landscape note](/Users/jb/Dev/automan/rnd/research/2026-10-07-commercial-landscape.md).

**Search and retrieval APIs for agents**
- **Exa**: "pay-as-you-go... no subscription and no minimum spend". New accounts get $10 of free credits that reset monthly. Search is priced per request, contents per page and Agent runs in compute units (or a fixed price per effort level). Enterprise offers custom indexes, SLAs, ZDR and postpaid invoicing — [Exa pricing](https://exa.ai/pricing). Websets is a high-compute asynchronous search product — [Exa Series B post, 2025-09-03](https://exa.ai/blog/announcing-series-b).
- **Parallel**: "priced per request, not per token... You always know the exact cost of a query before you run it". Processor tiers scale compute per task, 5,000 requests per month are free, and enterprise adds ZDR, DPA and SSO — [Parallel pricing](https://parallel.ai/pricing).
- **Tavily**: credits per operation; a free tier of 1,000 credits per month; pay-as-you-go per credit; credits reset monthly and requests stop "until your credits reset or you upgrade" — [Tavily pricing](https://www.tavily.com/pricing).

### Inferences
- Four archetypes map onto Goodfetch Cloud's three businesses. (1) Apify-type platform plus marketplace: prepaid usage, CU-like units and a commission. (2) Firecrawl and Spider-type crawl API: credits per page or bandwidth plus compute. (3) Browserbase, Steel and Kernel-type hosted browsers: time or memory-time, plus proxies, plus captcha. (4) Exa, Parallel and Tavily-type retrieval: per request. Each archetype has rivals with different meters. One engine that counts all the primitives (requests, bytes, browser-seconds, tokens per model, solver attempts, proxy bytes) lets Goodfetch offer **each business's familiar unit** on a common ledger, rather than forcing one unit on all three.
- Under `MIT OR Apache-2.0`, Goodfetch cannot copy the Browserless or Lightpanda move of selling a self-hosting license (anyone may self-host for free). The enterprise "private deployment" demand those vendors monetize has to be sold as **operations**: a managed dedicated or customer-cloud fleet, support and SLAs, licensed Private components (for legal-exposure items only) and compliance artifacts. That is consistent with [ADR 0018](/Users/jb/Dev/automan/docs/adr/0018-permissive-license-private-components-only-for-legal-exposure.md). Only Anchor (customer cloud), Kadoa (on-prem or private cloud), Lightpanda and Browserless (on-prem or private deployments) were found selling such deployments, and Browserbase explicitly does not do on-prem. A BYOC fleet would therefore be a differentiator, not table stakes.
- Enterprise tiers are packaged around the same set everywhere (ZDR, SSO and SCIM, SLAs, HIPAA BAA, DPA, static egress IPs, invoicing). These are "operations" features that fit ADR 0018's cloud-only perimeter.
- LLM resale already has three competing structures: pass-through at cost (Browserbase Model Gateway; Hugging Face, §3), cost plus 20% (Browser Use), and no resale at all (Spider bills the user's own provider). Proxies are either bundled (Kernel: "no charges... for proxies"), billed per GB (Apify, Browserbase, Steel, Browser Use) or billed in units per MB (Browserless).
- Data products are the access companies' upsell: Bright Data Datasets, Zyte Data, Firecrawl Alexandria. A Recipe marketplace can be the Goodfetch analog (curated, maintained extractors), but a datasets line raises the controller-side GDPR questions in §7.

## 2. Revenue or ARR, growth, customers, headcount, funding, valuations and acquisitions (with dates)

### Takeaway
There are two financial profiles. **Bootstrapped, profitable access and data businesses** have disclosed or filed numbers: Apify about $31M ARR at end-2025 [P], Bright Data above $300M annualized revenue in November 2025 growing over 50% [D], Oxylabs $350M ARR in July 2026 [D] with a 47.6% pre-tax margin at its Lithuanian entity in 2025 [filing via press]. **VC-funded, agent-era API and browser companies** raise at high valuations while disclosing almost no revenue: Exa $2.2B (May 2026), Parallel $2B (April 2026), Browserbase $300M post-money (June 2025), Firecrawl $75M Series B (September 2026), Kernel $22M (October 2025), Browser Use $17M seed (March 2025). Exits so far include Tavily to Nebius ($275M, about 98x 2025 revenue per a Sacra-derived figure) and ScrapingBee to Oxylabs (eight figures).

### Inferences
- A realistic, evidence-based trajectory for an EU-based, developer-led scraping platform is Apify's: it survived a failed raise and layoffs in 2022 and reached ~$13M revenue in 2024 and $31M ARR by end-2025 while profitable. AI demand roughly 2.4x'd its run rate in a year [C: $13M 2024 revenue to $31M end-2025 ARR, not like-for-like]. Goodfetch's realistic band for years 1–4 is the Bessemer "Shooting Star" curve (~$3M → ~$100M ARR), not the Supernova curve.
- Access networks are the most profitable sub-segment: Oxylabs UAB went from a 15.9% to a 47.6% pre-tax margin between 2023 and 2025 [filing via press], and Bright Data calls itself "highly profitable". That profit comes from operating and governing a proxy network (§7: KYC, compliance), which is costly to replicate. **Managed proxies** in Goodfetch Cloud are therefore more plausibly a resold or partnered input than a built network.
- Investors price agent-era retrieval and browser infrastructure on narrative and developer adoption: Exa at $2.2B and Parallel at $2B with no disclosed revenue, Tavily bought at ~98x revenue [Sacra-derived]. These are not useful revenue benchmarks for Goodfetch. Apify, Bright Data and Oxylabs are.
- Consolidation is under way: proxy groups buy self-serve API brands (ScrapingBee, Webshare) and AI clouds buy agent search (Nebius–Tavily). This favors a Goodfetch model whose moat is the cloud's operations and marketplace, not a single API.

## 3. Gross margins and cost structures of usage-based infrastructure, and the share of cost going to proxies, LLMs and compute

### Takeaway
Public usage-based infrastructure companies earn **49% to 87% GAAP gross margin**. Network and compute operators cluster between 57% and 76%: Fastly 57.1%, DigitalOcean ~60%, MongoDB 72%, Snowflake product 72%, Confluent 74.3%, Cloudflare 74.5%, Elastic 76%. Twilio sits at 49% because carrier pass-through fees sit in its cost of revenue, the closest analog to resold proxies and LLM tokens. GitLab sits at 87% because it is mostly seat software. Margins fall as the hosted share rises (MongoDB, GitLab) and rise with scale efficiencies (Fastly, Elastic). AI-heavy products run lower: 50–60% in a16z's 2020 sample, about 60% for Bessemer's "Shooting Stars" and about 25% for "Supernovas". The only private scraping disclosure is Oxylabs UAB's 47.6% pre-tax margin in 2025, which implies a much higher gross margin. Pass-through inputs (proxy bandwidth, LLM tokens, captcha solves) are the main swing factor in a scraping cloud's gross margin.

### Inferences
- Benchmark for Goodfetch Cloud's blended GM. Browser and crawl compute on rented clouds should land where DigitalOcean, Fastly and Snowflake sit (~60–75%) once utilization is high; marketplace commission revenue is near 100% margin; resold proxies, LLM tokens and solver attempts behave like Twilio's carrier fees and drag the blend toward 50% unless marked up or bundled. The expert-standard move is to report two numbers internally: GM on operated compute and GM on pass-through inputs.
- Pricing structure follows from the cost structure. Charging LLM tokens at cost plus a stated percentage (Browser Use's 20%) and proxy bytes per GB keeps margins positive on the volatile inputs. Bundling proxies into compute time (Kernel) is simpler for buyers but exposes margin to bandwidth-heavy targets. Goodfetch's per-unit engine counters make either choice possible per business.
- MongoDB and GitLab show that margins compress as the hosted share grows and third-party hosting costs scale. A self-host-first open-source framework will see blended margins decline as the cloud succeeds, unless fleet efficiency (Elastic's hosting-cost efficiencies, Fastly's utilization) keeps pace.
- Agent features (building, repairing and running scrapers with LLMs) carry the AI-margin profile (25–60% per Bessemer). Replaying compiled deterministic plans instead of looping an LLM is the main lever; the local corpus cites "$0.50–3.00 → $0.01–0.05 per task" [V] — [pricing-signals.md](/Users/jb/Dev/automan/rnd/research/market/pricing-signals.md).

### Gaps
- No scraping, browser or agent company discloses the split of its cost of revenue between proxies, LLM tokens and compute. Apify's, Bright Data's and Zyte's gross margins are not public.
- The Oxylabs registry figures were not read first-hand (HTTP 403); the filing's cost-of-sales line, which would give a gross margin, was not obtained.
- Cloudflare's reason for its 2.8-point GM decline in 2025 was not checked.

## 4. Open-source-to-paid funnel benchmarks: conversion, self-serve ARPU, cost of a free tier, time to first revenue

### Takeaway
No credible benchmark ties **GitHub stars to paying customers**: the widely repeated "0.5–3% of open-source users convert" is an unsourced heuristic. The best measured benchmark is ChartMogul and ProductLed's 2026 report: median free-to-paid conversion of 8% within six months; freemium "good" 3–5% and "great" 8–12%; card-required trials around 30%. Company datapoints show that conversion of *active* users is far higher than conversion of *sign-ups*: Apify had 27,000 paying customers against 56,000 active users at end-2025, while claiming 10k+ sign-ups a day. Free tiers are now small and capped in currency terms ($1–$30 equivalents). Two infrastructure companies (Heroku, PlanetScale) removed theirs, citing abuse and profitability.

## 5. Marketplace economics: Apify Store payouts, commission rates in comparable marketplaces, and what makes them work or fail

### Takeaway
The Apify Store shows that a scraper marketplace can carry real money. It paid developers **$1.6M in the last month** (page fetched 2026-10-12), across 4,500 community developers and 85,421 Actors, up from more than CZK 24M (≈$1M [C]) for all of 2024. The split is 80% to the developer **net of platform costs**, which Apify bills to the developer. Apify retired flat monthly rentals in favor of pay-per-event in 2026. Comparable take rates run from 0% (Hugging Face Inference Providers, Shopify's first $1M) through 1.5–3% (AWS Marketplace) and 15% (Shopify above $1M) to 25% (Rapid). The API-only marketplace (RapidAPI) declined, while marketplaces that own the runtime and the bill (Apify, AWS) hold.

### Cited Findings
- **Apify Store**: "$1.6M paid out last month", 4,500 community developers, 85,421 tools, "Many developers earn over $3k" [V] — [Apify partners](https://apify.com/partners/actor-developers). Nokia bought the business in November 2024 at an undisclosed price after a $1B valuation — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/13/nokia-acquires-rapid-the-api-company-once-valued-at-1b/).

### Inferences
- Implied Apify Store scale [C]. Because a developer keeps 0.8 × revenue minus platform costs, $1.6M per month of payouts implies at least ~$2.0M per month of Store billings (≥$24M annualized) as a lower bound. Apify also collects the 20% commission and the platform usage that developers pay. Set against Apify's $31M ARR at end-2025, this suggests the Store has become a large share, plausibly the majority, of Apify's billings. Confidence is low: the dates differ (Dec 2025 vs Oct 2026), "last month" may be a peak, and the ARR definition is unknown. Payouts grew roughly 19x in under two years (≈$1M for all of 2024 against ≈$19M annualized in October 2026) [C, exchange rate ~CZK 23 per USD].
- What makes the Apify model work, from the evidence. (1) The marketplace owns the runtime and the bill: users pay platform usage plus developer fees from one prepaid balance, so developers cannot be disintermediated. (2) Usage-based monetization (PPE) beat flat rental: Apify killed rental, and PPE covered 97% of Actors. (3) The platform absorbs free-user costs and handles tax and payouts. (4) Analytics and promotion: the Insights dashboard, a $1M challenge, $500 of creator credits. (5) KYC on developers for agentic payments. Rapid shows the failure mode: an API listing layer without the runtime, where buyers and sellers can transact directly once they meet.
- Commission benchmark for Goodfetch Recipes: 20% net of platform costs is Apify's proven point. Platforms with distribution power (Shopify above $1M, Atlassian-like stores) sit at ~15%, enterprise procurement channels (AWS) at 1.5–3%, and pure API brokers (Rapid) at 25%. The defensible choice is Apify-like (usage pass-through plus a 15–20% commission) with a 0% or low introductory tier like Shopify's to seed supply.
- Payment architecture: a marketplace rules out Paddle and Stripe Managed Payments for the marketplace flows. Goodfetch would act as the platform (Stripe Connect with KYC'd developer accounts) and handle VAT itself, for example with Stripe Tax at 0.5% per transaction (§7). Apify's "we handle hosting, billing, payments, and taxes" promise to developers is part of the product.

## 6. Pricing structures users reward or punish (2025–2026 evidence), and outcome-based or per-verified-result pricing

### Takeaway
2025–2026 evidence reinforces the local corpus. Users punish **opaque unit changes** (Cursor's June 2025 switch to usage credits ended in a public apology and refunds), **uncapped bills** (Netlify's $104.5k DDoS bill, forgiven only after it went viral), **expiring credits and feature multipliers** (still present at Apify and Firecrawl). Vendors are converging on **success-only billing**: Zyte, Bright Data Unlocker and SERP, Spider, partly Firecrawl, and Cloudflare's pay-per-crawl, which charges only on 2xx responses. They are also adopting **never-expiring pay-as-you-go credits** (Browser Use, Spider, Exa with no subscription), **known-in-advance prices per request** (Parallel) and **spend limits** (Firecrawl, Zyte, Apify, Hugging Face orgs). Outcome-based pricing is established in AI customer service (Intercom Fin at $0.99 per outcome; Zendesk per verified resolution). In scraping, "success" is defined at the HTTP or result-count level; **no vendor was found that bills per semantically verified result**.

### Cited Findings
- **Cursor**: in June 2025 Cursor replaced request caps with a usage credit pool at API rates ($20 of usage for Pro). Users ran out quickly and were charged overages without a spend limit set. CEO Michael Truell apologized ("we didn't handle this pricing rollout well") and offered refunds for unexpected usage between June 16 and July 4, 2025 — [TechCrunch, 2025-07-07](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/07/cursor-apologizes-for-unclear-pricing-changes-that-upset-users/); [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jul/5/cursor-clarifying-our-pricing).
- **Netlify**: in February 2024 a DDoS on a free static site produced a $104,500 bandwidth bill. Support first offered 20%, then 5%; the CEO forgave the bill after the story went viral. There was no spend cap and no alert — [Netlify forum](https://answers.netlify.com/t/netlify-billing-horror-story/113392); [Hamy](https://hamy.xyz/blog/2024-03_avoid-side-project-financial-ruin).
- **Local corpus (desk research, 2026-10-08)**. Complaints: credit multipliers, billing for failures, no spending cap (bills ~40x expectations; a $40 overnight retry loop), bandwidth billing, expiring credits, compute units at volume, gated access and KYC, price jumps (free to $2,500/month), and opaque agent bills. Accepted or requested: payment per successful result, per accepted lead or record, PPE, "1 credit = 1 scrape" with no multiplier, pay-as-you-go and small packs, caps per task or run or tool, and cost per accepted result as an internal indicator — [pricing-signals.md](/Users/jb/Dev/automan/rnd/research/market/pricing-signals.md).
- **Still-present punished structures**. Apify's prepaid usage "expire[s] at the end of the billing cycle" — [Apify](https://apify.com/pricing). Firecrawl credits do not roll over (except Scale for one month); JSON, Question and Highlight formats add 4 credits per page; a 403 or 404 page costs 1 credit — [Firecrawl](https://www.firecrawl.dev/pricing). Tavily credits reset monthly — [Tavily](https://www.tavily.com/pricing).
- **Success-only billing**. Zyte: "only charged for successful Zyte API responses" — [Zyte docs](https://docs.zyte.com/zyte-api/pricing.html). Bright Data Web Unlocker and SERP API charge only successful requests and do not bill failed retries — [Bright Data docs](https://docs.brightdata.com/scraping-automation/web-unlocker/introduction). Spider: "If Spider returns an error, you are not charged" — [Spider](https://spider.cloud/guides/pricing-and-plans/). Firecrawl does not charge a scrape that returns no result — [Firecrawl](https://www.firecrawl.dev/pricing). Bright Data's Web Scraper API is "pay-per-result" — [Bright Data products](https://brightdata.com/products). Browserless charges captcha units "per successful solve" — [Browserless](https://www.browserless.io/pricing). Cloudflare's pay-per-crawl charges crawlers only on successful 200-level responses carrying a `crawler-charged` header — [Cloudflare, 2025-07-01](https://blog.cloudflare.com/introducing-pay-per-crawl/).
- **Never-expiring credits and no subscriptions**. Browser Use: "No subscription. Credits never expire." — [Browser Use](https://browser-use.com/pricing). Spider: "Credits never expire" — [Spider](https://spider.cloud/guides/pricing-and-plans/). Exa: "no subscription and no minimum spend" — [Exa](https://exa.ai/pricing). Firecrawl's pay-as-you-go credits stay until used but "expire if you cancel" — [Firecrawl](https://www.firecrawl.dev/pricing).
- **Predictability and caps**. Parallel: "priced per request, not per token... You always know the exact cost of a query before you run it" — [Parallel](https://parallel.ai/pricing). Firecrawl's auto-recharge runs up to a monthly spend limit the user sets — [Firecrawl](https://www.firecrawl.dev/pricing). Zyte suspends the account after the trial until a spending limit is set — [Zyte docs](https://docs.zyte.com/zyte-api/pricing.html). Apify bills paid users only up to a configured usage limit and gives agent tokens a fixed spend cap — [Apify](https://apify.com/pricing). Hugging Face org admins can set a spending limit — [HF](https://huggingface.co/docs/inference-providers/pricing). Cloudflare crawlers can declare a `crawler-max-price` up front — [Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/introducing-pay-per-crawl/).
- **Price competition**. Browserbase cut its Developer plan 50% (from $39 to $20 per month) and raised included concurrency in June 2025 — [Browserbase](https://www.browserbase.com/blog/series-b-and-beyond). Zyte reassigns site tiers quarterly with two weeks' notice; reviewers flag that buyers often do not know the tier before scraping [E] — [Zyte docs](https://docs.zyte.com/zyte-api/pricing.html); [ZenRows benchmark](https://www.zenrows.com/blog/best-web-scraping-apis-benchmarked).
- **Outcome-based pricing elsewhere**. Intercom Fin at $0.99 per outcome, billed "only when Fin successfully resolves a customer conversation end-to-end" [V] — [Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/learning-center/ai-customer-service-agent-pricing-comparison). Salesforce Agentforce at $2 per conversation, charged whether or not the issue is resolved, or Flex Credits (~$0.10 per action), described as "nearly impossible to forecast" [E] — [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/is-salesforce-agentforce-worth-the-cost). Zendesk prices per verified resolution [E] — [Intercom comparison](https://www.intercom.com/learning-center/ai-customer-service-agent-pricing-comparison). The definition of "resolution" is the main buyer risk — [same](https://www.intercom.com/learning-center/ai-customer-service-agent-pricing-comparison).

### Inferences
- The structure the evidence rewards, for each Goodfetch business, is **success-only metering at the unit the buyer recognizes**, with a hard cap and credits that do not expire while the account is active. In practice: verified results or pages for the crawl API; events or results for Recipes (Apify PPE); browser time for hosted browsers (failed or idle time unbilled, as at Kernel); requests at a price known in advance for agent tasks (Parallel).
- "Per verified result" goes one step beyond the market. Competitors bill HTTP success (Zyte, Bright Data, Spider) or result counts (Bright Data, Apify PPE), not semantic correctness. Outcome-based pricing in AI support shows buyers accept a premium per outcome when "outcome" is defined and auditable, and that disputes center on the definition. Goodfetch's lab contracts (`C-zero-false-success`, `C-cost-per-1k-verified` in [pricing-signals.md](/Users/jb/Dev/automan/rnd/research/market/pricing-signals.md)) are therefore the commercial prerequisite, not just an engineering metric. Without an auditable verifier, per-verified-result pricing would invite Agentforce-style disputes.
- Charging pass-through inputs separately (proxy GB, model tokens at cost plus x%) is accepted when it is visible and capped. The corpus complaints target hidden multipliers, not visible line items.
- Agentic buyers are a new billing persona: Apify's x402/MPP tokens with fixed spend caps and Cloudflare's 402-based pay-per-crawl show machine-to-machine payment with a declared maximum price. A per-run budget declared by the caller (agent or human) is the cap mechanism that fits both.

### Gaps
- No public data was found on churn or conversion effects of specific pricing changes in scraping (for example, before and after Browserbase's price cut).
- No vendor in this market was found selling per semantically verified record; willingness to pay a premium for verification remains unmeasured (the local corpus also flags this).
- Firecrawl's 2025–2026 pricing changes and any related backlash were not researched before the search budget ran out.

## 7. Legal and compliance operating costs that shape the model, and payment providers or merchant-of-record options for an EU-based seller

### Takeaway
Compliance is a real operating cost and a conversion filter. Since 2026-07-07, Bright Data allows **no self-serve path to its residential network**: every new zone requires a corporate email, funds on the account, a registration document, human review and possibly a video call. US case law currently favors logged-out scraping of public data (Meta v. Bright Data, 2024; X v. Bright Data dismissed in 2024, then settled in 2025; Ryanair's jury verdict against Booking overturned in 2025). Contract, fake-account and data-handling claims still bite (hiQ paid $500k and accepted an injunction in 2022). In the EU, the AI Act bans facial-image scraping for recognition databases (from 2025-02-02, fines up to €35M or 7% of turnover), and GPAI providers, likely Goodfetch customers, must honor text-and-data-mining opt-outs (from 2025-08-02). GDPR enforcement reaches scraped contact data (CNIL fined Kaspr €240k in 2024). On payments, an EU seller can be its own merchant on Stripe (~1.5% + €0.25 for standard EEA cards, plus 0.7% Billing and 0.5% Tax) or use an MoR at about 3.4–5% plus a fixed fee. MoRs exclude marketplaces (Paddle prohibits them; Stripe Managed Payments does not support them) and Paddle restricts proxies.

### Cited Findings

**KYC, acceptable use and abuse handling**
- Bright Data residential network: "There is no automatic, instant or self-serve path to the Residential network". KYC applies to new residential zones created after 2026-07-07 and requires a corporate email (no personal accounts), funds added (not available in trial modes) and human review ("Approval is never automatic"). The process involves a KYC form with the use case and a certificate of incorporation, plus possibly a government ID and a "mandatory" video call when requested, during which the team may view the customer's systems; updates come within 48 hours. Requests outside the approved use case return "Access denied", and government websites are blocked on all networks. ISP and datacenter proxies and Web Unlocker remain available without KYC — [Bright Data docs](https://docs.brightdata.com/proxy-networks/residential/network-access).
- Apify requires developer KYC for agentic-payment eligibility — [Apify docs](https://docs.apify.com/platform/actors/publishing/monetize). Browserbase sells "Verified Agents" at Scale — [Browserbase pricing](https://www.browserbase.com/pricing). Cloudflare's pay-per-crawl requires crawler registration and Web Bot Auth: an Ed25519 key published as a JWK and requests signed with RFC 9421 HTTP Message Signatures. Cloudflare acts as MoR between crawler and publisher; the program was in private beta at publication — [Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/introducing-pay-per-crawl/).
- Local corpus: users complain about KYC and gated access ("accounts blocked at sign-up") — [pricing-signals.md](/Users/jb/Dev/automan/rnd/research/market/pricing-signals.md).

**Scraping litigation**
- **Meta v. Bright Data**: on 2024-01-23, Judge Edward Chen (N.D. Cal.) granted summary judgment to Bright Data on breach of contract, holding that it "only engaged in logged-out scraping of public data" — [Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/post/102kqw7/). Meta dismissed its remaining claim on 2024-02-23 and waived appeal; Bright Data said there was no settlement — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/26/meta-drops-lawsuit-against-web-scraping-firm-bright-data-that-sold-millions-of-instagram-records).
- **X Corp v. Bright Data**: Judge William Alsup dismissed the complaint on 2024-05-09. He found access-based claims did not plausibly allege harm, and found the scraping-and-selling claims preempted by the Copyright Act — [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/judge-tosses-xs-contract-claims-against-data-scraping-company/). Bright Data countersued X on antitrust grounds — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/rkgkhxxr1x). The parties reported a settlement in principle in June 2025, and the docket shows termination on 2025-07-01 — [CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/67637345/x-corp-v-bright-data-ltd/) (per search-result extraction).
- **Ryanair v. Booking.com** (D. Del.): a July 2024 jury found a CFAA violation with intent to defraud, but returned exactly $5,000 of "loss" — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/ryanair-wins-jury-verdict-in-scraping-case-against-booking-com). On 2025-01-22 the court granted Booking's renewed motion for judgment as a matter of law, finding Ryanair had not proven the $5,000 loss threshold. The court also held that the CFAA applies extraterritorially, a holding commentators call controversial. The summary-judgment stage had followed hiQ in holding that access to public data is not a CFAA violation, while fake accounts or false information could be — [Eric Goldman blog](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2025/03/court-overturns-a-bad-jury-verdict-against-scraping-ryanair-v-booking-guest-blog-post.htm).
- **hiQ v. LinkedIn**: a consent judgment and permanent injunction (court order 2022-12-08) with a $500,000 judgment against hiQ; hiQ must stop scraping, may not use fake accounts and must destroy code, data and algorithms derived from scraped profiles. A November 2022 ruling had found LinkedIn's anti-scraping and fake-account terms enforceable in contract. The stipulated conclusions carry no precedential value — [Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2022/12/08/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/380493/web-scraper-agrees-to-pay-linkedin-500000-destr.html).

**EU AI Act and GDPR**
- AI Act Article 5(1)(e) prohibits AI systems that "create or expand facial recognition databases through the untargeted scraping of facial images from the internet or CCTV footage", applicable from 2025-02-02 — [Art. 5](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/). Fines reach €35M or 7% of worldwide turnover (whichever is higher; for SMEs, whichever is lower) — [Art. 99](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/99/).
- Article 53(1)(c) requires GPAI model providers to adopt a copyright policy that identifies and complies with rights reservations under Article 4(3) of Directive 2019/790 (text-and-data-mining opt-outs), and 53(1)(d) requires a public training-content summary. Both apply from 2025-08-02 — [Art. 53](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/53/). The GPAI Code of Practice, including a Copyright chapter, was published on 2025-07-10. Signatories include Amazon, Anthropic, Google, IBM, Microsoft, Mistral AI and OpenAI; xAI signed only the Safety and Security chapter — [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/contents-code-gpai).
- **GDPR**: CNIL fined KASPR €240,000 (decision 2024-12-05, announced 2024-12-19). KASPR's paid extension revealed the contact details of LinkedIn users, including those who had restricted visibility, from a database of about 160 million contacts. The main breach was lack of a legal basis (Article 6) — [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/en/data-scraping-kaspr-fined-eu240000). The EDPB adopted Opinion 28/2024 on personal data in AI models on 2024-12-18 — [EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/opinion-board-art-64/opinion-282024-certain-data-protection-aspects_en) (substance not extracted).

**Payments and merchant of record (EU-based seller)**
- **Stripe as own merchant** (France): standard EEA cards 1.5% + €0.25; premium EEA cards 2.8% + €0.25; UK cards 2.5% + €0.25; international cards 3.15% + €0.25, plus 2% if currency conversion is needed; SEPA Direct Debit €0.35; disputes €20. Add-ons: Billing 0.7% of billing volume (pay-as-you-go), Invoicing 0.4%, Tax 0.5% per transaction (or €0.45 via API), and **Managed Payments (MoR) 3.5% per transaction on top of payment fees** — [Stripe pricing (FR)](https://stripe.com/en-fr/pricing). Billing's usage-based features run through the Meters API, "with up to 100M events per month included". Advanced usage billing is a separate product, Metronome — [Stripe Billing pricing](https://stripe.com/en-fr/billing/pricing).
- **Stripe Managed Payments**: Stripe becomes merchant of record and handles indirect tax in 80+ countries, fraud, disputes and transaction-level customer support. France and most EU countries are eligible seller locations. It covers digital products (SaaS, PaaS, IaaS, AIaaS tax codes); it excludes B2B services involving humans and Connect platforms; the product must be sold directly, not through a platform or marketplace. Stripe may issue refunds within 60 days to limit disputes — [Managed Payments](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments); [eligibility](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/eligibility).
- **Paddle**: MoR at 5% + 50¢ per checkout transaction, including tax, fraud, chargebacks and buyer support; custom pricing for high volume; bespoke pricing for products under $10 — [Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing). Its policy prohibits marketplaces and lists "VPN and Proxies" as a restricted category with enhanced due diligence. Products enabling "unauthorized access to data belonging to another party" or infringing "terms and conditions" (including "content copying") are prohibited, which could catch some scraping uses — [Paddle policy](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/what-am-i-not-allowed-to-sell-on-paddle).
- **Lemon Squeezy**: 5% + 50¢; its footer reads "Sold through Link, LLC f/k/a Lemon Squeezy LLC", and a banner announces "2026 Update: Lemon Squeezy + Stripe Managed Payments" — [Lemon Squeezy pricing](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/pricing).
- **Polar**: MoR plans from Starter (free, 5% + 50¢) to Scale ($400 per month, 3.4% + 30¢); +1.5% for international cards; $15 per dispute regardless of outcome; transaction fees not returned on refunds; Polar may refund up to 60 days after purchase; payouts at Stripe cost (0.25% + $0.25 per payout, plus $2 per month) — [Polar fees](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/fees).
- **Stripe restricted businesses**: proxies, VPNs and scraping are not listed. "Platforms that host or distribute third-party content" is a restricted category requiring extra information, and Stripe "may not be able to allow" such businesses — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses).

### Inferences
- Operating-cost lines the model must carry, ordered by how strongly the evidence supports them. (1) **Customer KYC and use-case review** for anything that sells residential or mobile proxies or solving. Bright Data's July 2026 tightening suggests upstream proxy suppliers will require it from resellers, which makes KYC a pass-through obligation and a self-serve friction point. (2) **Acceptable-use enforcement**: per-domain or per-category blocking of government sites, logged-in targets under hostile terms and facial-image collection (AI Act Art. 5(1)(e)), with audit trails. (3) **Robots.txt and TDM opt-out compliance** as a product feature, because GPAI-provider customers must prove compliance under Art. 53. (4) **A legal reserve and counsel** for platform-holder claims: Bright Data's wins still meant years of litigation, and hiQ shows that fake accounts and terms breaches lose. (5) **GDPR controller exposure** for any Goodfetch-run datasets or caches, as the Kaspr fine shows. As a processor running customers' Recipes, Goodfetch needs DPAs and use-case terms.
- Expert-default payment structure for the three businesses. The marketplace cannot use an MoR (Paddle prohibits marketplaces, Stripe Managed Payments excludes them), so Goodfetch is the merchant: **Stripe Payments + Billing (meters) + Tax + Connect** for developer payouts. Indicative take: ~1.5% + €0.25 (EEA card) + 0.7% + 0.5% ≈ 2.7% + €0.25 per transaction, before Connect payout fees [C]. An MoR adds roughly 3.5 points (Stripe Managed Payments) or costs 3.4–5% + a fixed fee (Polar, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy) [C]. It would only fit the plain API subscriptions, and Paddle's proxy restrictions and data-access clauses make it a poor fit for a scraping cloud.
- Pay-per-crawl and Web Bot Auth point to a future where reputable crawlers authenticate and sometimes pay publishers. That is a new cost line (publisher fees, as in Firecrawl Alexandria's paid provider agreements) and a possible product line: signed crawler identity plus publisher payments relayed through Goodfetch's billing.
- The US cases support Goodfetch's design choice of logged-out public data by default and explicit customer responsibility for logged-in Recipes. The EU adds the AI Act and GDPR obligations above, so an EU-based seller should budget compliance as a fixed cost from day one rather than as a later enterprise add-on.
